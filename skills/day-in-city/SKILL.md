# day-in-city: one good day, in an order that works

A day out fails in ordinary ways: the main sight is closed that weekday, the route crosses town twice, lunch falls where nothing is open, and the slowest person is worn out by two o'clock. This skill builds the day around the actual date and the actual people. No country is the default: ask where, and look up local hours, prices, currency, units and customs each time.

## Ground rules

- **Never assume a fact about a place.** Opening hours, closed days, last entry, prices, ticket and booking rules, and step-free access change. Label every one: **checked** (page or lookup tool, date read; only what you opened or looked up in this session counts), **from memory, to check** (you are sure the place exists but could not read the fact today), or **unconfirmed** (the place exists, but you are not sure it is still running, open to visitors or right for this party). When most facts share a label, say so once above the route and mark only the exceptions.
- **No invented places.** Every place named carries one of the three labels. Build the route only on checked places and on well-known ones you are sure exist; an unconfirmed place can only be an optional extra. If you doubt a place exists, leave it out.
- **Sources.** The venue's own site first, then the city or national tourism office, the transit operator and the official weather service. Customs, dress rules for religious sites and safety notes come only from those or from government travel advice, never from rumour about an area or its people.
- **The stated pace and needs are limits, not hints.** Never plan more than the slowest person can do. Take mobility and access needs exactly as stated, without guessing more or less; call a route or venue step-free only if the venue or operator says so. If an access need cannot be checked, say so at the top, put that check first in the to-check list, and give a fallback that needs no check (a taxi, a street-level backup).
- **Book nothing, pay nothing, contact no one.** Add calendar entries only if asked.

## Steps

**1. Get the day.** Take what the message gives. You need: the place; the date; start and end points and times; who is going (ages, mobility, energy); interests; budget; food needs; pace. If the place or the date is missing, ask for those in one short message and wait; write no route until you have them, because everything depends on where and closed days depend on the date. If only the weekday is known, plan for it and say so. Assume the rest and list the assumptions on top: 09:30 to 18:00 (a half day is four to five hours), start and end at the same central point, two adults plus any children named, steady pace, mid budget, and meals outside the time window already eaten. A need given without a number ("bad knee", "needs a rest after lunch") gets the careful reading, stated as an assumption: short slow walks for the knee (about 500 m each, then a seat or a ride); for the rest, one hour, taken on the route if a quiet indoor seat exists and otherwise back at the start point. Say which you assumed. If the person is away or cannot answer: with no place, stop at the question; with a place but no date, plan for the next day that fits their words (tomorrow if they give none) and say so; assume everything else, say so, and go on.

**2. Look up the date.** Weekday; any public holiday, weekly rest day, festival, strike or big event; sunrise and sunset; the forecast if the date is within its range, otherwise the usual weather for that month, labelled as such. Then, for each candidate stop: hours on that day, last entry, price, whether it needs a timed ticket, and access notes. If lookups are few, spend them on the main stops' hours first and put whatever you could not look up (holiday, events, strikes) in the to-check list. Times you work out yourself, such as sunset, are estimates.

**3. Choose the main stops.** A full day holds 2 main stops at an easy pace, 3 at a steady pace, 4 at a full pace; a half day holds 1 or 2. Choose ones that are open that day, match the interests, and sit in one area or two neighbouring ones, so the day is spent in places and not between them. Meals and breaks are not main stops, and two sights side by side count as one. Add up to three small extras that lie between main stops and can be dropped without harm. An extra's time comes out of the unplanned hour, and the route says so; a half day has no such hour, so give an extra there only if the times still fit with it, and show its minutes. Never put a needed break or meal inside an extra.

**4. Put them in order.**
- The most crowded stop, or the one with a timed ticket, goes first thing, at opening. Late-closing places go last.
- Outdoor stops get the best light and weather; indoor ones take midday heat or the likeliest rain.
- Alternate heavy and light. Never two museums in a row, and no more than about two to three hours in one place without a break.
- Meals fall at the usual local times, on the route. Check when local kitchens open and close.
- If sunset falls inside the day, a viewpoint or waterside stop can meet it. End near the end point.

**5. Time it.** Give an arrive and a leave time for every stop.
- Take travel times from a map or the transit operator's planner if you can; otherwise estimate and mark them. For walking, plan 4 km an hour for adults and 3 with small children, older or tired walkers, add time for hills, and say the distance in the local unit.
- Add slack to every leg: a quarter of the travel time, never less than 10 minutes. Slack is time for pausing, waiting and getting lost, not more walking, so measure a stated walking limit against the walk alone, and end each walk at a seat when a limit was given. A leg with a ride in it is one leg; add the usual wait for the ride to its travel time. Round times to the next five minutes.
- At a major sight, count the first 15 minutes of the stop as getting in (queue, bags, tickets).
- Keep one sit-down break besides meals in each half day, and one unplanned hour in a full day. With young children, keep nap and meal times fixed and plan around them; with a child under about six and no limit stated, keep each walk to about 1 km and name a ride or buggy fallback.
- For a day trip to another town, the journey out and back is part of the day. Check the return timetable for that date, name the last service back and the one before it, and plan to be on the one before; if the timetable cannot be read, say so and put that check first. A timetabled ride gets its wait plus 10 minutes of slack, not a quarter. If the journey leaves under about two hours in the town on a half day, say so at the top and let the day run longer, to about two hours there; do not cut the visit to nothing. A start or end time the person stated still holds: lengthen at the other end, and if neither can move, say plainly what fits.
- Add it up. If the day does not fit the time window, the walking limit or the pace, drop an extra, then a main stop. Do not shrink the slack.

**6. Food.** Name where and when to eat on the route, with one backup nearby and a shop or market option. Check the place is open that day and whether it takes bookings. Match diet needs. For an allergy, say plainly that you cannot confirm how a kitchen works and that they must ask the venue.

**7. Book ahead.** List what needs a ticket or table in advance, by when, and the official page. If something is sold out or cannot be confirmed, give the swap.

**8. Rain or tired version.** Say which outdoor stops swap for which indoor ones, what to cut first, and where the day can end early with the way back from there.

**9. Costs.** Per person and for the group, in the local currency: tickets, transport, food. Mark estimates. Mention a day pass or free-entry day only if checked. For a low budget, prefer free stops and a picnic or market lunch.

## What to give back

All ten parts, each as short as it can be, in this order:

1. **The day**: place, date and weekday, who, pace, assumptions, daylight and weather.
2. **If you only do three things.**
3. **Timed route**: time, stop, how long, how to get to the next (distance, minutes, slack), and a label for each fact. Extras marked "optional".
4. **Eat**: where, when, backup.
5. **Book ahead**: what, by when, where.
6. **Rain or tired version.**
7. **Costs.**
8. **Good to know**: dress rules, customs, safety and access notes, each with its source; if none could be sourced, say so in one line.
9. **To check before you go**: every "to check" and "unconfirmed" item as a short list, most important first.
10. **Sources**: pages read, with the date.

## When a tool is missing

- **No web access, or no page loads.** Do not retry and do not stall. Plan from what you know, name only well-known places, and label each place and fact "from memory, to check". Give hours and prices only as rough, labelled figures, and leave out safety and customs notes you cannot source. Put the checks that could break the day first in "To check before you go": is each main stop open on that weekday, last entry, and whether it needs a ticket in advance. Say at the top that nothing was checked live. If only some pages fail, do not retry them; label those facts "from memory, to check" and keep "checked" for what did load.
- **No map or transit tool.** Estimate from distance with the walking speeds above and mark every leg "estimate".
- **No calendar tool, and entries were asked for.** Give the times as a plain list to copy.
- **A small or little-documented town.** Fewer confirmed stops is fine. Plan around what can be confirmed and tell the person to ask at the local tourist office.

## Several days in one place

Plan one day at a time by these rules. Use the closed days to decide which stops go on which day; where they decide nothing, give each day one area. Give the assumptions, daylight and weather, "Good to know", the to-check list and Sources once for the whole stay, and for each day only the three things, timed route, eating, booking, rain or tired version and costs. Flights, stays and preparation are not this skill's job.

## Worked example (place and venues invented; shortened, a real reply has all ten parts)

> We have Saturday free in Port Calder with our kids, 6 and 9. Staying at the Gull Street guesthouse. One of us is vegetarian. Not too rushed.

No date was given, but the weekday was, so plan for a Saturday and say so. Assumptions on top: this coming Saturday, 09:30 to 18:00, from and back to the guesthouse, two adults and two children, easy pace with a 6-year-old (3 km an hour, walks under 1 km with the harbour bus as the fallback, 2 main stops: the museum and the gardens), mid budget.

- **Three things**: the Tidewater Museum's hands-on boat hall, the harbour ferry, the Saltmarket for lunch.
- **09:30** leave the guesthouse; 900 m walk, 18 min plus 10 slack, arrive 10:00.
- **10:00 to 12:00** Tidewater Museum (main), at opening, before the crowds; sit down with a snack about 11:00. Open Saturday 10:00 to 17:00 and family ticket price (checked: museum's "Visit" page, read today).
- 400 m, 8 min plus 10 slack. **12:20 to 13:20** lunch at the Saltmarket food hall; vegetarian stalls listed on the market's own page (checked); backup: the museum cafe (from memory, to check).
- 300 m, 6 min plus 10 slack. **13:40 to 14:25** harbour ferry loop: the afternoon's sit-down break, not a main stop. Saturday timetable (checked: ferry operator's page). Optional extra: the lighthouse (unconfirmed whether it opens to visitors; 20 min, taken from the free hour).
- 500 m, 10 min plus 10 slack. **14:45 to 16:00** Calder Gardens (main), playground and paths, outdoors in the driest part of the forecast. Free (from memory, to check).
- 700 m, 14 min plus 10 slack. **16:25 to 17:25** unplanned hour at or near the guesthouse.
- **Book ahead**: nothing must be booked; museum tickets online skip the queue (checked, same page).
- **Rain or tired**: swap the gardens for the museum's upper floors or the covered market; cut the lighthouse first; the ferry pier is 10 minutes from the guesthouse if the day ends early.
- **Costs**: tickets, ferry and lunch per person and for four, in local currency, estimates marked.
- **To check before you go**: museum cafe hours; gardens entry; whether the lighthouse opens.
