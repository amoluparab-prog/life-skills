# big-buy: a major household purchase, decided on use, evidence and full cost

Choosing by a shop page's star rating or a "best of" list that earns commission is a common way to buy badly. A good buy comes from three things: how this person will really use the item, what independent testing says, and what it costs over the years they keep it. Work through the steps in order and give one report.

## Rules that hold throughout

1. **Never invent** a model, spec, price, test result or review. If you neither saw it in this session nor were told it by the person, it is "not checked".
2. **Every price carries where and when it was seen** ("649 at a national electrical chain's site, 22 Nov"). If the page shows its own older date, or someone else reported the price, say that too and call it stale. Prices and stock change, so say so once. Never write "lowest" or "best price" as a fact; write "lowest seen in this check, across N sellers", and try to see the recommended pick at two sellers or more.
3. **Evidence is labelled by kind**, strongest first: *independent test* (a consumer organisation or lab that takes no advertising and is independent of makers and sellers), *owner survey* (reliability data from many owners), *reviewer* (a specialist site that explains how it tested), *shop reviews* (use only for repeated fault patterns, never the star score), *seller claim only*. Print the label beside each piece of evidence. Anything else (a seller's own guide, a review with no stated method, a forecast): name what it is and treat it as seller claim only. Nothing read: write "none read". Say when seller claims are all there is. A result behind a paywall is "exists, not read", never guessed.
4. **No steering.** Naming where a price was seen is a source, not a recommendation. A plain link to the page where a price or fact was seen is fine, with any tracking tags stripped; no affiliate links, no "buy here".
5. **Rights are general.** Describe return and warranty rights in plain terms and point to the official consumer body for their country (or state or province, where rules are set there). This is not legal advice.
6. **General knowledge is allowed but labelled.** What kind of product suits a use, usual sale seasons, or a consumer body's name may come from memory if you mark it "general knowledge, not checked today". Models, specs, prices and test results may not (rule 1).
7. **"None found" means you searched.** If you did not search for something, write "not searched".
8. **No country is the default.** Use their country's currency, units, labels and sale seasons. Do not buy, reserve or sign up for anything.

## Step 1. Get the essentials

Use what the request already says. Then ask, once, at most four short questions (one or two facts each), only for what is missing and would change the answer; assume the rest and say so. The essentials:

- what they are buying and why now (broken, moving, upgrade);
- which country they are in, and whether they will buy online or in a shop;
- how they will really use it: who, how often, for what, and what annoyed them about the last one;
- budget and currency, the space it must fit (and the route in), hard limits, and how many years they expect to keep it.

If they are away or say "just tell me", do not ask. State your assumptions in two or three lines at the end of the report and carry on. If the country is unknown and nothing in the message shows it (a currency, a place, a shop), do not guess one or its currency: give step 2, step 4 with their numbers (a worksheet with blanks if they gave none), step 5 as urgency and the "was"-price warning only (no sale seasons), step 7 without a named body, a pick between any models they named, and say the shortlist and the rights need their country. If they asked one narrow question (such as "which of these two is cheaper to own"), answer it and cut the other steps to a line each.

## Step 2. What matters for them

Name the three or four things that decide this purchase for their use, each tied to something they said and given as a number or a test ("9 kg drum because you wash bedding for five weekly"). Then name two or three features they can ignore and why. Size and fit to the space come first; a wrong size cannot be fixed later.

## Step 3. Shortlist by live search

Search their market for current models that meet step 2 within budget. Budget about twelve lookups (searches and page reads both count); a page that is refused, fails or returns the wrong content is not retried, it is marked "could not open". Spend them in this order, and mark whatever is left over "not searched": (1) the independent consumer testing body in their country, then owner reliability surveys, then specialist reviewers; if the first search shows no testing body for their country, try at most one nearby country's that tests the same model codes, say so, and move on to prices; (2) price and stock for each model, at two sellers for the likely pick; (3) the energy label figure or other main running cost; (4) recalls and price history. Shortlist three or four models; two well-checked models beat four thin ones. For each give:

- exact model name and code (codes differ by country; check it is sold there);
- price seen, where and when, whether delivery is included, and whether it was in stock;
- evidence, with its label from rule 3;
- known faults or recalls found, or "none found in this check";
- warranty length and who gives it; repair and spare-parts support if stated.

If they said they are open to it, include one refurbished or previous-year option; if you cannot ask, spend no lookups on it and mention it under "what would change the answer". An option with no price seen is "not checked", not a shortlist entry. If nothing can be verified, give fewer models, or none, and say so.

## Step 4. Cost over its life

For each shortlisted model, show the sum line by line so they can redo it:

`price + delivery + installation + removing the old one + needed accessories + (yearly energy, water and consumables x years kept) + likely repairs - resale value = total; total / years = cost per year`

- **Years kept:** their own figure. If none, take a round figure, say it is an assumption and show how the result moves with a shorter life.
- **Energy:** the kWh figure on the official energy label x their own price per kWh. Check the label's basis (per year, per 100 cycles, per 1,000 hours) and scale it to their use. No label: watts x hours a day / 1,000 x 365 x price per kWh, with watts from a spec page seen and hours from the person. Ask for their tariff or look up a typical one and label it. Assumed watts, hours or tariff make the line "illustration only", kept out of the total.
- **Efficiency classes or stars** compare only like with like (same type and size); kWh compares across any.
- **Unknowns stay unknown.** A cost you could not find is listed as "not known", never as zero and never guessed. Count repairs or resale only with evidence.
- **Running costs without a label** (servicing a bicycle, filters, ink, blades): use a price seen or one the person gives; otherwise "not known", with the seller question that would fill it.
- **Optional extras** such as an extended warranty stay out of the totals; show each as its own line so its effect is visible.
- **Label figure not seen:** give that model's total without energy and say so. You may add one line marked "illustration only: if its figure matched model X", so the person can see the size of the gap. An illustration never goes in the headline without that mark.

## Step 5. Buy now or wait

Urgency first: if the old one is dead, say what waiting costs them, and recommend only something seen in stock. Otherwise look up, for their country, when this kind of product is usually refreshed and its usual sale periods, and any price history for the shortlisted models. Treat crossed-out "was" prices with suspicion; a regulator's sale-season sweep found "was" prices the item had recently sold at, and prices raised before a sale. Give a target: "a good price is at or under X, because it sold at X on [date, source]". No history found: say so and give the lowest seen in this check instead; if you saw only one seller, say it is just the price seen, not a target.

## Step 6. The picks

- **Recommended:** the model, the reason in their terms, its lifetime cost.
- **Cheaper pick:** what they give up (a feature, evidence, or certainty of getting it).
- **Skip:** a model or feature tier that looks tempting, and why.

If the search verified no current model that fits, do not force a name: state the pick as a rule ("whichever has X, at or under Y") and list the models to check. Say what would change the answer (a different budget, a sale, a test result you could not read).

## Step 7. Before paying and on delivery

- **Ask the seller:** is this the exact model code; total delivered and installed price; will they match a price seen elsewhere (worth asking, most of all on large appliances); return window and who pays for return; who handles a fault, the seller or the maker; how long spare parts are kept; what any extended warranty adds beyond their legal rights and the maker's warranty (often little).
- **On delivery:** measure the route in beforehand; inspect before signing; photograph any damage and note it on the delivery slip; check the model code on the box; test every function in the first days; keep the packaging until the return window ends; keep the receipt and register the warranty.
- **Rights to confirm:** the return window for online and for shop purchases, the legal guarantee period for faults, and whether paying by card adds protection. Name the official consumer body for their country and tell them to confirm there.

Fit these lists to the item and to how it is bought: a bicycle has a frame size and model year, not a model code, and a shop collection has no delivery slip or box. If they will buy in a local shop whose prices are not online, online prices are a reference to take along, not the price to expect.

## Report order

Recommendation first, in three lines. Then: what matters, shortlist table, lifetime cost with sums, buy now or wait, picks, seller questions and delivery checks, then "not checked", "not searched" and assumptions. If they asked for a quick take, keep the same order but cut each part to its few most useful lines.

Follow-up questions after the report: answer from what was already seen and say so; make new lookups only for what the new question needs.

## If a tool is missing

No web search: open with one line saying nothing was looked up, then the recommendation. Still do steps 2, 5 in general terms (rule 6) and 7, and do step 4 with the person's own numbers, or as a worksheet with blanks. Name no model as a current pick from memory. For step 6, pick between the models the person gave you, or state each pick as a rule as step 6 describes. List the exact searches for them to run. If they gave you models and prices, work from those and label them "as you told me".

## Worked example

A session with no search tool. The person writes:

> "Which of these is cheaper to own? Washer A is 480, label says 52 kWh per 100 cycles. Washer B is 390, 88 kWh per 100 cycles. I pay 0.30 per kWh, do about 5 washes a week and keep machines 10 years. Delivery is free on both."

They gave prices, use and years and asked one narrow question, so ask nothing. Do the sums before you write the headline, so the two cannot disagree; the reply still opens with the headline. No country or currency shows, so name none. Outline of the reply:

> Nothing was looked up for this; it is worked from your numbers.
>
> **A is cheaper to own, by 190.80 over 10 years, on price and energy alone.** Not checked: test results, reliability, water use. Any of them could outweigh that.
>
> 5 washes x 52 weeks = 260 washes a year.
> A: 52 / 100 x 260 = 135.2 kWh x 0.30 = 40.56 a year; x 10 = 405.60; + 480 = **885.60** (88.56 a year).
> B: 88 / 100 x 260 = 228.8 kWh x 0.30 = 68.64 a year; x 10 = 686.40; + 390 = **1,076.40** (107.64 a year).
> A costs 90 more to buy and saves 28.08 a year, so it pays back in a little over 3 years. Kept only 3 years, B is cheaper by 5.76.
>
> Check both have the drum size you need and fit the space. No rush unless the old one is dead; judge any sale by prices you have seen, not the crossed-out one. Ask the seller the exact model code and who handles a fault.
> Prices and label figures are as you told me. Not known: water, repairs, resale. Not searched: an independent test of each model [the exact searches]. Tell me your country and I will name the consumer body and the rights to confirm.
