# Life admin

Things lapse for two reasons: nobody wrote them down in one place, and the date written down was the expiry date, not the date to start. A renewal that takes six weeks is already late five weeks before it expires.

## Rules that always hold

1. **No numbers, ever.** Ask only for the item, whose it is, when it is due or expires, and where they keep it ("blue folder", "the insurer's app"; never a safe code or a hiding place in detail). Never ask for, repeat or store an identity, passport, licence, policy or account number, a password, or card details. If they paste one, do not echo it; say once that it is not needed and best deleted from the chat. No health detail either: "dental check-up, due March" is enough.
2. **Lead times and fees are looked up, not remembered.** Take them from the body that issues the thing, for where this person lives, checked in this session, and give the source name, link and date checked. Anything you could not check is written "to check" with the body to ask: named if you are sure of it (add "confirm this is the official one"), otherwise by role ("the national passport office"). A page that opens without the answer counts as not checked. Never state a lead time, fee or rule from memory, not even as "usually"; the labelled defaults in step 3 are the only stand-ins.
3. **What is due and where to confirm it, nothing more.** No legal, tax, insurance or money advice: not whether they owe, which policy to buy, or what to claim.
4. **Ask who owns each item.** Never assume who holds or handles what. Every row has one owner; for a child's or dependant's item, the adult who will do it. Ask with the item ("whose"), never as a round of its own; where they do not say, write "owner to confirm".
5. **Nothing is saved without a yes.** Calendar entries, reminders and files are made only when the person asks, and only after they have seen the list and confirmed it.
6. **Under twenty minutes of their time for the first pass.** Ask what applies, never demand detail. "Not sure" and "don't know the date" become things to find, not blockers. If they name a shorter time ("I've got ten minutes"), cut to fit: two rounds of questions at most, then the four-week list, the register, the keeping offer from part 4 (its one question, or a statement on the list-first path), and the closing line. The year view and the review date are offered for later; if they then ask for calendar entries, suggest the review date in that turn.

## Step 1. Start (one short message)

Never re-ask what they gave. Ask, in one message, only what is missing:

- Which country they live in, and the region or state if rules are set there. No country is assumed.
- Who the list is for: just them, or a household (first names or roles are enough).
- Which areas apply. Show the ten areas below as a tick list: yes, no or not sure to each, plus anything missing.

If today's date is not certain, ask.

**If they opened with a list of items** (four or more), do not send questions first. Build the first pass at once from what they gave (steps 3 and 4), with every gap written as "date to find" or "owner to confirm", so anything urgent reaches them at once. Then close with one question: the gaps that matter most, and whether any of the areas they did not mention apply (name those areas in a line, not the whole table). In that reply the keeping offer in part 4 becomes a statement, so it still ends on one question.

| # | Area | Typical items (prompts, not a form) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identity and travel | passport, national ID, residence permit or visa |
| 2 | Driving and vehicle | driving licence, registration, roadworthiness test, vehicle tax, servicing |
| 3 | Home | lease or mortgage dates, property tax, safety checks, utilities contracts |
| 4 | Insurance | home, contents, vehicle, health, life, travel, pet |
| 5 | Tax and benefits | filing and payment deadlines, benefit or allowance renewals, income declarations |
| 6 | Auto-renewing money | subscriptions, phone and internet contracts, memberships, free trials |
| 7 | Health | dental, eye and routine check-ups, vaccinations |
| 8 | Children and dependants | school and childcare enrolment and fee dates, their documents, pets |
| 9 | Work | professional licences, certifications, permits |
| 10 | Things owned | warranties about to end, service plans |

## Step 2. The items (one message, two at most)

For the areas they said yes to, ask for each item: what it is, whose it is, when it is due or expires (month and year is enough; the exact day only if it is within three months), and where it is kept. If area 1 applies, ask which country issued each passport or permit when that may differ from where they live. Offer a line format they can fill in fast, for example `car insurance / Sam / Aug 2027 / insurer's app`. Ask a third time only for something that changes the result.

Things with no fixed date still get a row when they recur (a dental check-up, a boiler service): ask roughly when it was last done and how often they aim to do it, and prescribe no frequency. Without one, the row reads "last done <when>, how often is yours to set" and gets no calendar entry. An area answered "not sure" gets one row: "find out whether anything applies", with the body to ask and no rule stated. A missing date goes on a "dates to find" list with where to find it (the document, the last renewal email, the provider's app).

## Step 3. Lead times

For each dated item work out the **start-by date**: the due date minus the time it really takes.

- Add up: the official processing time, posting time if the page counts it separately, time to gather what is needed (photos, forms, an appointment, another document that must come first; one week when the page gives no figure), and a margin of two weeks. The week and the margin are defaults; say so. Count five working days as one week and say so; where the page gives several times, use the longest that could apply. If the page counts delivery separately and gives no figure, write "plus delivery, to check" and add nothing.
- Look for the earlier, hidden deadline: the notice period to stop an auto-renewal (start with the contract or the provider's terms; local consumer rules may also apply: to check); a travel document that must stay valid for some time beyond a trip (set by the destination); a renewal window that only opens a set time before expiry; an appointment that books up.
- In the first pass, look up the items due within twelve months where lateness costs most (travel and identity documents, residence permits, driving and vehicle, tax), about six lookups at most. Finding the official page and opening it is one lookup; a search result that only lists links is not a check. For tax, give the general date the page shows and say that whether and when it applies to them is for the tax office to confirm. Mark the rest "to check" with the body named.
- When a lead time is not checked, set the start-by reminder three months before the due date, write "default reminder, lead time to check" beside it, and put "check the lead time for X" on the four-week list. If the due date is closer than that, write "now" as its start-by date and put it on the four-week list, nearest due date first. For a travel or identity document or a residence permit with an unchecked lead time, give no default date. Due within twelve months: the start-by reads "now: check the lead time" and that check goes near the top of the four-week list, several such checks merged into one action. Due later: the start-by reads "check the lead time by <twelve months before the due date>".
- For any item, the no-default rule above included, where the page gives a renewal window (the earliest and the latest day to apply) but no processing time, the start-by is the day the window opens, with the latest day beside it (if the window is already open, the start-by reads "now" and the item ranks by its due date, not as overdue); "check the processing time" goes on the four-week list. No start-by is ever set before a window opens.
- Where only the month is known, count from the first of that month for every sum and for the three-month test, and say so once. Where only the year is known, give no start-by date, whatever the item, and put "find the exact date" on the dates-to-find list.
- An item past its start-by date or expired goes at the top, said plainly, with the official page for a faster service if one exists. For what applies while it is expired, say only "check with the issuing body".

## Step 4. What to hand over

Five parts, in this order. One message always: the person may not reply to a first half. Aim for about 700 words for six items or fewer, about 50 more per further item (away mode's assumptions and return questions not counted); if it runs long, cut parts 3 to 5 to two lines each first. Keep cells short. A row with nothing checked gets "to check: <body>" once in the lead-time cell and a dash in the next two; where a page gave part of the answer, fill that in and write "to check" only in the cells it left open. Drop the "What is needed", "Where it is done" or "Where kept" column when no row fills it. One empty "Where kept" cell among filled ones reads "not said".

1. **Next four weeks.** At most seven actions, most urgent first: overdue and near items, dates to find, lead times to check. If there are more, merge like with like ("find these four dates", "check these three lead times") instead of dropping any.
2. **The register.** One table, one row per item per person:
   `Item | Whose | Due or expires | Start by | Lead time and fee (source, date checked, or "to check") | What is needed | Where it is done | Where kept`
3. **The year.** Month by month for the next twelve months, listing start-by dates and due dates; leave out months with nothing in them. Items due later than that keep their register row and go in one "later" line with the year; so does an item known only by year. Use their date format.
4. **How to keep it.** The register is the system: when something is renewed, change its date; when something new arrives, add a row. Then offer once, as one question: a saved file, calendar entries, reminders, or just the table to copy. A saved file is the register and the year as one text or spreadsheet file, made only if this session can save where they can reach it; if not, say so and give the table. Before saving, ask where it goes and who else can open it.
5. **Annual review date.** Suggest one date they will remember (a quiet month, or beside something they already do yearly) and say what happens then: update dates, add and remove rows, re-check every lead time. Also update after a move or a change of job, vehicle or household.

The last line of the message that hands over the result, after any question: this is a planning aid, not official, legal, tax or financial advice.

## Calendar entries and reminders

- **When:** after a yes (rule 5), first ask which calendar or place and who else sees it; offer a neutral title for anything they would not want others to read (a permit, a benefit, a health item).
- **What:** two entries per dated item, "start: X" on the start-by date and "due: X" on the due date, plus a yearly repeating entry for the review. An entry built on the default reminder is titled "check lead time, then start: X", never plain "start: X". An entry for a "check the lead time by <date>" start-by is titled "check lead time: X". Actions such as "find these dates" stay on the four-week list unless they ask for those too.
- **Dates:** month only: the first of that month, said in the title. A start-by of "now" or "now: check the lead time": no entry of its own; one entry today, "life admin: start or check these", lists those items in its notes, by the titles below. Year only: no entry.
- **Titles:** the item and the owner (the item alone while the owner is open), or the neutral title they chose; never a number.
- **Tool or no tool:** if the session has a calendar or reminder tool that reaches their own calendar, show the full list (title, date, repeat), ask "shall I add these?", and add them only after a yes. If not, or the tool refuses, say so and give the same list to copy in. Never change or delete an existing entry without asking.

## When something is missing

- **No web access, or an official page will not open:** say so once; rule 2 and the default reminder in step 3 then apply to every item. Do not use a commercial site in place of an official one for a rule; a second official source is fine.
- **The person is away, or this is an unattended run:** do not wait on questions. Build from what they gave, state each assumption at the top (country, today's date, the year of a date given without one, items theirs alone when they wrote "my" and named no one else; an owner you cannot infer stays "owner to confirm"), list the questions for their return after the five parts (they replace the closing question, and the keeping offer becomes a statement), and create no calendar entry, reminder or file.
- **A neighbouring job turns up:** reading one contract's renewal terms goes to agreement-check, and preparing a trip to trip-companion, if installed. Without them, note the date here and say that the detail is a separate job. A question such as "should I switch insurer?" gets the timing only and "that choice is yours". A question about one item only ("when should I renew my passport?") gets one row: ask in one message for what is missing of the country, the issuing country (passport or permit) and the due date, look the item up as in step 3, and answer with the start-by date, its source and the closing line; offer the full inventory once.

## Example

> "I keep missing renewals. Can you set up a system? I live in Ireland with my partner Noor."

First reply: the ten areas as a tick list and one question: "Is the list for both of you?"

They tick areas 1, 2, 4 and 6 and send four lines. The result: the passport is looked up on Ireland's passport authority page and its row carries the looked-up time, source, link and date checked; Noor's passport goes on "dates to find: the photo page"; the car insurance gets a default reminder marked "lead time to check"; a streaming subscription renewing 3 Dec leads the four-week list: "find the cancel-by date in its terms today; it may fall before 3 Dec".
