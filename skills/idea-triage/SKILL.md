# Idea triage

A person with twenty ideas often works on none of them, or on the newest. This skill turns the pile into a short decision: one or two ideas to do, a few to test cheaply, the rest parked or dropped, with the reasons in view. It uses plain weighted scoring and borrows the lessons of RICE, ICE and kill criteria, including their known faults.

## Rules that hold throughout

Scoring tables look more certain than they are. These rules keep the result honest.

- **The person owns the weights and the final call.** You propose; they decide. If they overrule a verdict, record it as their choice and do not argue twice.
- **A guess never passes as a fact.** Every score carries a label: **S** (they said it), **L** (looked up, with the source named), **G** (your guess or theirs).
- **Invent no numbers about the world.** No market sizes, demand figures, prices or competitor counts unless they gave them or you looked them up and name the source. "Unknown" is an allowed answer.
- **Say when the list cannot be ranked honestly.** If one missing fact would reorder the top, name that fact instead of giving a confident order.
- **Nothing is deleted.** Parked and dropped ideas stay on a list with the reason.

## 0. Check fit

Use this for one person's own list of two or more ideas. If they have a single idea and want it examined in depth (bedrock, if installed) or pitched (working-backwards-pitch, if installed), want new ideas, or want a team's backlog, sprint or roadmap ranked, say this skill does not fit and help in the ordinary way. One exception: if a single idea turns out to be several, offer to triage the versions.

## 1. Collect and tidy the list

- Take the list as it comes: pasted, a file (read it), or one at a time. When gathering one at a time, say "give me all of them, rough is fine" and do not comment on any idea until they say that is all.
- Number the ideas and restate each in **one plain line**: what it is and who it is for. Keep their own name for it.
- **Merge** duplicates and ideas that are one idea in two forms. Say which were merged and why; the person can split them again.
- More than about twelve ideas: after step 2, set aside everything that fails a deal-breaker and ask the person to pick the ten or so they would be sorry to lose. Score those; the rest go on the parked list as "not scored", sharing one revisit date.

## 2. Agree the yardstick before scoring

Agreeing the yardstick first stops the scores being bent to fit a favourite. Send the tidy list with these questions in **one message**, numbered, and ask only for what the person has not already told you:

1. **Aim.** What are these for: income, learning, enjoyment, helping someone, a career move? Unlike ideas cannot be compared without it.
2. **Limits.** Hours a week, money they can lose, any deadline.
3. **Deal-breakers.** Anything that rules an idea out whatever else it has (for example "must not need a co-founder"). These are gates, not scores, so that small pluses cannot outvote a must-have. Something they cannot do ("I can't code") is a low Fit score, not a gate, unless they say it rules the idea out.
4. **What they already know.** For which ideas has anyone asked, paid or complained? Which one pulls them most? Without this, almost every score would be a guess.
5. **Criteria and weights.** Propose three to five from the menu below, with a suggested split of **10 points**. Ask them to change the split or add their own. If you do not know the aim yet, say the split is a first guess and adjust it when they answer. All are worded so that higher is better.

| Criterion | 3 means | 1 means |
|---|---|---|
| Serves the aim | leads straight to what they said they want | does little for it |
| Wanted | someone has paid or firmly committed (asking or complaining alone is a 2) | nobody has shown they want it |
| Cheap to test | a real test within two weeks for little money | months or real money before learning anything |
| Fit | they have the time, money and skills now | needs something they do not have |
| Easy to undo | little is lost if it fails | hard to reverse or costly to stop |
| Leaves room | blocks nothing else, or opens other ideas | shuts out the other ideas |
| Pull | they would start tonight | they feel they ought to |

For an idea with no customer (learning a language, writing for themselves), score "Wanted" 1 with a note and let "Serves the aim" and "Pull" carry it. Do not score "Wanted" on the person's own wish: that counts their enthusiasm twice.

## 3. Score

- Score each idea **1, 2 or 3** on each criterion. Coarse scores are deliberate: finer ones would claim a precision nobody has.
- Each score gets a **reason of a few words** and its **label** (S, L or G). "Wanted" scored on your own impression is G, however reasonable it sounds. If any part of the reason is a guess, or the person said nothing on the point, the label is G. If the idea as stated is bigger than its evidence (two cafes planned, one has asked), score the evidence.
- An idea that fails a deal-breaker is not scored: list it in the table as "not scored" with the gate it failed.
- With a search tool, a few quick lookups are worth it where a checkable fact would change a score (does it already exist, what a tool costs, whether a licence is needed). Name the source. Without a search tool, leave those as G and list them as things to check.
- Total = sum of weight x score (10 to 30). Totals **within 2 points are a tie**; never present a tie as a ranking.
- **Flip check.** Find the smallest change that would put a different idea on top: two weight points moved from one criterion to another, or one G score moved by one. If such a change exists, say the top place is not settled and what would settle it.

## 4. Verdicts

Give every idea exactly one. The totals inform the verdict; they do not make it.

- **Do now** - at most two, one unless the person has room for two. It passes every deal-breaker, sits in the top band (the top total or tied with it), its score on the heaviest criterion (or on each criterion tied for heaviest) is 2 or 3, is not a guess and does not rest on thin evidence, and more than half of its other scores are not guesses. If nothing qualifies, say so and give no "do now".
- **Test cheaply** - promising, but a heavy criterion rests on a guess or on thin evidence. At most three at a time; more than that and none gets finished.
- **Park** - worth keeping, not now. Give a **revisit date** (about three months on unless something points to another date) and a "sooner if" (the event that would bring it back).
- **Drop** - fails a deal-breaker on a fact, or scores low mostly on facts, or the person no longer wants it. An idea that is low mainly on guesses, or fails a gate only on something unchecked, is parked with the thing to check.

Within a tie, let the aim and the strength of the evidence decide which idea gets tested and which is parked, and say that is what you did. If the person overrules a verdict, mark it "(your choice)" and leave the scores as they are. If that makes a third "do now", ask which one moves down.

## 5. Cheapest next test

For each "do now" and "test cheaply" idea, give one test: the smallest step that would firm up its weakest point: a G on a heavy criterion, or an S that rests on thin evidence. State what to do, the cost in hours and money, a **pass line fixed in advance**, and a **date** ("by 14 June, 5 of 20 people asked say they would pay; if not, park it"). Fixing the line in advance matters because people stay with a failing idea too long once they have put work in. Pass lines are your suggestions, marked "(proposed)"; the person sets them.

## 6. The reply

In this order, with reasons kept to a few words so the verdicts fit on one screen: (1) the verdicts, one line each; (2) the tidy list with merges; (3) aim, limits, deal-breakers and weights used; (4) the scored table with reasons and labels; (5) how firm the order is: ties, the flip check, any missing fact; (6) next tests; (7) parked and dropped lists with dates and reasons; (8) one line saying they can change any weight or overrule any verdict and you will redo the table.

If you can write files and they would like one, save the whole result as `idea-triage-YYYY-MM-DD.md` so the parked list survives. Offer a reminder for revisit dates; create it only if they say yes.

## When the person is away or wants it in one go

Ask nothing, and work through these:

- Take the aim, limits and deal-breakers from what they wrote; where they said nothing, state your assumption in a line above the verdicts.
- Use Wanted, Cheap to test, Fit and Easy to undo, plus Serves the aim if they stated an aim, with **equal weights** (a plain sum of the scores, so ties are totals within 1). Label the criteria and the equal weights as your assumption, and show whether the top idea changes when one criterion counts double.
- For an idea with no customer, leave "Wanted" out of its total, say so, and compare it on its average score.
- Give no "do now" that depends on an assumed aim: make it "test cheaply" and say why. If they asked which to start with, still answer plainly: open with "Start with X, by running this test", so the missing "do now" does not leave them without an answer.
- With more than about twelve ideas, score them all with reasons of three or four words; do not choose which ones they would miss.
- Save no file unless they asked for one and create no reminder; put those offers in the closing lines. If a file they named cannot be read, say so and work from what is in the message.
- End with the two or three questions whose answers would most change the result.

## Example

"Rank my side-project ideas: (a) newsletter about local hiking trails, (b) trail-conditions app, (c) hiking blog, (d) selling my trail photos as prints. About 5 hours a week, I want some income within a year. Two friends keep asking me for trail tips. I write a bit already, I can't code, I have no online shop, and nobody has asked to buy a photo."

Tidy list: 1 Trail newsletter (merged with (c), the same writing in another wrapper); 2 Trail-conditions app; 3 Photo prints. Agreed weights: Wanted 4, Cheap to test 3, Fit 3.

| Idea | Wanted (4) | Cheap to test (3) | Fit (3) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Newsletter | 2, two friends ask, S | 3, one issue this week, G | 3, writes already, S | 26 |
| 3 Prints | 1, nobody has asked, S | 3, list five prints online, G | 2, has photos, no shop, S | 19 |
| 2 App | 1, no one has asked, G | 1, months to build, G | 1, cannot code, S | 10 |

Verdicts: 1 **test cheaply**, since two friends is thin evidence of "wanted"; test (about 4 hours, no money): send one issue to 20 hikers by 30 June; pass if 8 ask for the next, if not park it (proposed). 3 **park**, revisit 1 September, sooner if someone asks to buy a photo. 2 **park**, not drop, because its low "wanted" is a guess; revisit 1 September, sooner if newsletter readers ask for live conditions. No "do now" yet. Flip check: no single change moves 1 off the top.
