# Where to look things up

Use this for every fact that can change. The pattern matters more than the list: pick the source by its role, open the page itself, and give the traveller the source name, the link and the date you checked. If the page shows its own "last updated" date, give that too.

How to write a looked-up fact:

> Power banks must go in your carry-on, not your checked bag. Source: FAA PackSafe, lithium batteries page (page updated <the page's date>, checked <today's date>). Your airline may be stricter; its page says ...

If an official page will not open, try a second official source for the same rule (several are listed below for that reason); cite the one you read and say which you could not open. If none opens, say "not verified" and name the page to check. A search snippet, a travel blog, a forum or a booking site is not a source for a rule; use them only to find the official page.

Dates: prefer the date printed on the page ("last updated", "still current at"). If it shows none, write "no date shown". Always add the date you checked.

The entries below are starting points and may move. Those marked (†) are named from general knowledge: the author did not open them when writing this file, so find them by name and confirm the page really is the government's or the authority's own before relying on it. Those marked (‡) were confirmed only from a search listing under the official domain, not opened. The same check applies to any page: an official-looking name on a commercial visa or claims site is common.

## Entry rules, visas, advisories
Look these up for the **traveller's nationality** (the passport they will travel on), and for every country they enter **or change planes in**.

- The traveller's own government travel advice:
  - United States: travel.state.gov (country information and travel advisories)
  - United Kingdom: gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice
  - Canada: travel.gc.ca (travel advice and advisories) (‡)
  - Australia: Smartraveller (†)
  - New Zealand: SafeTravel (†)
  - India: Ministry of External Affairs and Bureau of Immigration (†)
  - Elsewhere: the foreign ministry of the passport country
- Getting home: if the traveller is not a citizen of the country they live in, that country's immigration authority page on re-entry for residents.
- The destination government's own immigration or embassy page, which has the final word on visas, electronic travel authorisations, passport validity, and blank pages. For the Schengen area: travel-europe.europa.eu (‡).
- Check both. If they disagree, or neither answers the question, say so and tell the traveller to confirm with the destination's embassy or the airline.
- Domestic trips: the security authority's page on accepted ID (in the US, TSA's identification page (†)).

Always check: visa or travel authorisation, how long the passport must be valid beyond the trip, proof of onward travel or funds, rules for children travelling with one adult, and the current advisory level.

## Health and vaccinations
- United States: wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel (by destination)
- United Kingdom: travelhealthpro.org.uk, and NHS Fit for Travel (†) (find the country by name and check the page heading; do not guess the address)
- Elsewhere: the national health ministry, or the World Health Organization's travel pages (†)
- Medicines: check on the destination government's or embassy's page that each prescription medicine is legal there. For anything beyond routine advice, tell the traveller to see a doctor or travel clinic, ideally four to six weeks ahead.

## Security: what may go in a carry-on or checked bag
Look these up for the **departure country**, and again for the return and any country where bags are re-screened on a connection.

- United States: tsa.gov ("What can I bring?"; its liquids-rule page was opened, but its item pages often refuse automated reading) and faa.gov/packsafe (batteries and other dangerous goods; its "medicinal and toiletry articles" page also gives the carry-on container limit for liquids, so it can stand in for that figure if a TSA page will not open)
- United Kingdom: gov.uk/hand-luggage-restrictions; the Civil Aviation Authority at caa.co.uk for batteries and other items allowed in baggage (‡); and the departure airport's own page, because liquid rules differ between UK airports
- European Union: the departure airport's page and the national civil aviation authority
- Canada: catsa-acsta.gc.ca ("What can I bring through airport security") (‡)
- Australia: homeaffairs.gov.au (TravelSECURE, "what you can and can't bring"), and casa.gov.au for batteries and dangerous goods (‡)
- India: Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (†)
- Elsewhere: the national civil aviation or aviation security authority, and the departure airport

## The airline's own rules
The airline's website for: cabin and checked bag sizes, weights and counts for this fare; power bank and battery limits (airlines may be stricter than the authority); when online check-in opens and closes; bag-drop and gate cut-off times; seat fees; change and refund rules. Check the airline that **operates** each flight, which on a codeshare booking may not be the one that sold it.

## Needs: allergy, diet, mobility, medical
The operating airline's own pages on special assistance, allergies and special meals (and how far ahead to ask); the airport's assistance page; the destination government's page on food allergen labelling if an allergy matters; the embassy's page on bringing medicines in.

## Dates and prices
School holidays: the school's or the local council's own term-dates page. Public holidays: the destination government's page. Prices: a live fare or room search, or the operator's own page (railway, attraction). If none could be had, the figure is an estimate and is labelled so.

## Passenger rights when a flight is delayed or cancelled
These are pointers to where the rule is; which flights each one covers is itself something to read on the page, not to state from here.
- United States: transportation.gov/airconsumer (the author opened a different page on the same site, not this one)
- European Union, Iceland, Norway, Switzerland: europa.eu/youreurope (air passenger rights)
- United Kingdom: caa.co.uk/passengers
- Canada: otc-cta.gc.ca (Air Passenger Protection Regulations) (‡)
- Elsewhere: the national civil aviation authority or consumer regulator

## Customs: what to declare
The customs authority of the country being entered, for example US Customs and Border Protection (†), GOV.UK "bringing goods into the UK" (†), the Canada Border Services Agency (†), the Australian Border Force (†), and the customs page of the destination.

## Weather
The national weather service for the destination (for example the US National Weather Service or the UK Met Office (†)), or a weather tool if the session has one. A forecast is only useful from about ten days out; before that, use climate averages for the month and say that is what they are.
