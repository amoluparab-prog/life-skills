# Phase 8: At the airport

Purpose: get from the kerb to the seat calmly, and know what to do if the flight goes wrong. Keep replies short here; the traveller is on their feet reading a phone.

## The usual order
1. **Bag drop or check-in desk.** Have documents out. Check the bag tag shows the right final destination, and keep the receipt. On a connection, ask whether bags are checked through.
2. **Security.** Liquids bag and electronics out if this airport asks for that (rules differ); empty pockets; medication and baby items declared if required. Assistance or family lanes exist at many airports; ask.
3. **Passport control** on international departures where it applies.
4. **Gate.** Check the screens, not just the boarding pass, because gates change. Note the time boarding closes, which is earlier than departure. Fill water bottles after security. Stay within reach of the gate once boarding is near.
5. **Boarding.** Boarding pass and ID ready. If a carry-on is taken at the gate, take out medication, valuables, documents, and spare batteries and power banks first.

## If the flight is delayed or cancelled
General steps. What the traveller is owed depends on the region, the airline and the cause, so do not promise an outcome; point to the airline and to the official passenger-rights page for the region (see `official-sources.md`) and look up the current rule before quoting any figure.

1. **Find out what is happening:** the airline's app and the airport screens, then the airline's staff.
2. **Act on two channels at once:** join the desk queue and use the app, website or phone line. Rebooking seats go quickly.
3. **Know the usual choices:** a later flight or another route to the destination, or a refund if they no longer want to travel. Accepting one may rule out the other; read before tapping accept.
4. **Ask what care is provided** during the wait (meals, a hotel, transport) and get the answer in writing or a screenshot.
5. **Keep evidence:** boarding passes, messages from the airline with times, receipts for anything they pay for.
6. **Tell whoever is affected:** the hotel (late arrival, so the room is not released), the transfer, the person meeting them. Then check the insurance policy's delay cover.
7. **A missed connection on one ticket** is generally the airline's to fix; go to its transfer desk. On separate tickets it is not, so contact the second airline before that flight leaves.
8. **Bag missing or damaged on arrival:** report it at the airline's baggage desk before leaving the hall, and keep the reference number; look up the airline's deadline for a written claim.
9. **Refused boarding because the flight is oversold:** ask for the reason in writing and what the airline offers; the passenger-rights page for the region says what is owed. **Missed the flight through their own lateness:** go to the airline's desk or app straight away; what it will do depends on the fare, so ask before buying a new ticket.

If the first message is a cold "my flight is cancelled", lead with what to do in the next ten minutes (steps 1 to 4, a line each; if it is evening, sorting tonight's bed with the airline comes before the desk closes), then one line on rights with the official passenger-rights page for the region they are departing from, and for the airline's home region if that differs (which one applies is read on the page), then ask only what they have not said: which airline and flight, where they are, whether it is one ticket. Aim for about 250 words; they are reading on a phone. Before they buy a new ticket on another airline themselves, tell them to ask the airline first and to read the regional rule, because getting that money back is not certain.

Update the trip sheet and offer to move the calendar entries once the new plan is known.

## Done when
They are on the plane, or they have a new plan and know what to claim and where.

## Dated items to offer
The new flight times, any claim deadline.
