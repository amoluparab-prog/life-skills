# Phase 1: Shape the trip

Purpose: agree what the trip is before any money is spent, and find anything that could stop it.

This phase is too big for one message on the full track. Split it: first the blockers and the dates, then the rough plan and the budget, then the wrap-up with the trip sheet.

## Steps
1. **Check the blockers first.** Look up, for each traveller's nationality and each country entered or transited (see `official-sources.md`):
   - visa or electronic travel authorisation, and how long it takes to get
   - passport validity required beyond the trip, compared with the month and year the traveller gave
   - vaccinations or health entry requirements
   - the current travel advisory
   - rules for children travelling with one parent or with other adults
   - **getting home again:** if a traveller is not a citizen of the country they live in or return to, look up on that country's immigration authority page what they need to be let back in (residence permit or visa valid on the return date, any re-entry permit, rules on time spent abroad). Ask for status only, never a document number.
   - a visa or residence permit they already hold can change the entry and transit answer, so ask whether they hold one before quoting a visa rule
   - for any stated need: whether their medicines and medical devices are legal to bring in, and what the destination and the airline offer for an allergy, a diet or a mobility need
   The route is not known yet, so say that the country they change planes in will be checked in phase 2. If the traveller's own government page is silent on a point, say "not verified" and send them to the destination's embassy or the airline.
   State each with source and date. If a passport or visa cannot be sorted in time, say so now and offer other dates or destinations.
2. **Destination fit.** If the place is fixed, check it against their purpose, the travellers' needs and the time of year (weather, school holidays, local holidays, closures). If it is open, offer two or three options with one line each on why it fits and the main drawback. Do not write a guidebook.
3. **Timing.** With flexible dates, say which days or weeks are likely cheaper or quieter and why; mark anything not looked up as a general pattern.
4. **Rough daily plan.** One line a day: where they sleep and the one main thing. Leave arrival day and the day after a long flight light. Allow for the slowest traveller (small children, older relatives, mobility needs).
5. **Total budget.** A simple table: flights, stays, local transport, food, activities, insurance, visas and fees, and a 10 to 15 percent cushion. Use looked-up prices where you can (a fare search, the rail operator's or attraction's own page) and mark every other figure as an estimate; a "from" teaser price on an advert is not a looked-up price. Compare the total with the budget they gave and say plainly if it does not fit, with what could be cut.

## Done when
The traveller has said yes to: destination, dates (or a date window), the rough plan and the budget, and knows of any visa, passport or health step with its deadline.

## Dated items to offer
Visa or authorisation application deadline, passport renewal start date, vaccination or travel clinic appointment, the date to have an answer on medicines.
