# Phase 5: Packing

Purpose: a list made for this trip, and no surprises at security or the bag drop.

## Build the list
1. **Start from the plan**, day by day: what will they actually do (meetings, hiking, a wedding, swimming), and how many days before laundry is possible.
2. **Look up the forecast** for the dates and places (see `official-sources.md`). More than about ten days out, use climate averages and say so, then offer to re-check nearer the date.
3. **Write the list in groups**, with numbers, for each traveller: documents and money; clothes by type and count; shoes; toiletries; medication and first aid; electronics and chargers, adaptor for the destination's sockets; items for children; items for the purpose (work kit, gifts, sports gear). Mark which bag each group goes in.
4. Always in the **carry-on or on the person**: documents, wallet, phone, medication, glasses, valuables, a charger, spare batteries and power banks (size and number limits come from the lookup below), anything they cannot do without for 48 hours if the checked bag is delayed, and a change of clothes for children.
5. If an event needs a particular outfit and an event-outfit skill is installed, hand that part over and fold the answer in.

## What may go in which bag
Rules differ by country, airport and airline, and they change. Look up both, every time, and cite source and date:
- the **aviation security authority of the departure country** (and of the return and any connection where bags are screened again)
- the **airline's own baggage page** for this fare

Check each of these:

| Item | What to look up |
|---|---|
| Liquids, gels, creams, aerosols | Size limit per container and total in the carry-on at the departure airport; whether that airport has different rules; exemptions for medicine and baby food; duty-free bought on the way |
| Batteries and power banks | Spare lithium batteries and power banks: cabin or hold, watt-hour limit, how many per person, whether the rating must be printed and readable on the unit, whether the airline is stricter, where it must be kept on board, and whether it may be used or charged there |
| Sharp items and tools | Scissors, razors, knives, tools, sports gear: carry-on or checked only |
| Medication and medical devices | Carrying liquid medicine, needles, devices; whether a prescription or letter is needed; whether the medicine is legal at the destination |
| Food, plants, animal products | What the destination's customs allows in |
| Sizes and weights | Cabin and checked bag dimensions, weight and number for this fare, and the fee for going over |

Keep the lookups in proportion: the departure country's authority and the operating airline first. For the return and any connection where bags are screened again, look them up when the traveller is packing for real or asks; until then say in one line that those rules may differ and are not yet checked (if you happened to read the return rule on a page you opened anyway, give it in that one line). If the operating airline is not known, answer for the airline named and say so.

### Answering "can I take this in my carry-on"
Get the departure airport, the airline and the date, look the item up on both sources, and reply in this order:
1. One sentence with the verdict for everything they asked about.
2. For each item: **Carry-on: yes / no / with conditions. Checked bag: yes / no / with conditions.** Then only the conditions that apply to them, in a few short lines.
3. Sources, one line each: name, link, the page's own date (or "no date shown"), date checked. Where the two sources differ, give the stricter condition.
4. One closing sentence that carries the caveats together, for example: "The officer at security has the final say, and rules on the way back may differ." Add "this is a lookup of published rules, not official advice or travel insurance" if it has not yet been said in this conversation. Then the one-line offer.

Aim for about 150 words an item before the source lines, and give only the tier their item falls in (no 160 Wh rules for a 74 Wh battery). Leave out conditions that plainly do not apply (when unsure, keep the condition), and caveats that are only theoretical (for example the operating-airline note when the airline they named flies the route itself).

Fixed advice that needs no lookup, and should be given as advice:
- Medicine or a device the traveller may need during the journey (an adrenaline pen, an inhaler, insulin) always stays with them in the cabin, whatever the hold rules allow. Look up only how to carry it through security and whether a letter or prescription is needed.
- If a carry-on is taken at the gate to go in the hold, take out medication, documents, valuables, spare batteries and power banks first.
- If a battery label shows only milliamp-hours, show the sum: mAh divided by 1,000, times volts. Use the voltage on the label; if there is none, use 3.7 V, the usual figure for these cells, and say it is an assumption and that they should check the label.

## Photo check
Offer once: "If you like, send a photo of what you have laid out, or of the packed bag, and I'll check it against the list and the rules."
- Compare what is visible against the list: say what you can see, what seems to be missing, and what looks like it is in the wrong bag (a power bank in the suitcase, a large bottle in the carry-on).
- Say what a photo cannot show: sizes in millilitres, weights, what is inside a pouch. Ask about those; do not guess.
- If documents or cards are in the photo, do not read out or keep any numbers from them, and suggest they retake it without them.
- If the session cannot see images, ask for a typed list.

## Done when
The traveller has a list per person and per bag, knows the limits for their bags, and every item they asked about has a looked-up answer or is marked not verified.

## Dated items to offer
"Re-check forecast" a few days out; "weigh bags" two days out.
