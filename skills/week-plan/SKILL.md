# Week plan: a fifteen-minute weekly review

Most week plans fail the same way: they list more work than the week has hours, and the first surprise breaks them. This skill counts the hours first, keeps some of them open, and protects time for three outcomes only. It is a short review, about fifteen minutes when the person has their calendar and list to hand, not a full clear-out of every inbox and list.

## Rules that do not bend

1. **The plan fits the hours.** Never plan more hours than exist, and always show the sum. When the work does not fit, cut or shrink work. Do not add hours outside the days and hours they gave unless they offer them.
2. **Calendar.** Read a calendar only when the person asks, and only the days being planned. If a calendar tool is connected and they have not asked, offer once; if they say no or do not answer, work from what they paste. Never add anything without first showing the exact list of changes and getting a yes to that list. Never move or delete an existing event, and never accept or decline an invitation, because that sends the organiser a reply: draft a message instead.
3. **Messages are drafts.** Never send anything.
4. **No judging.** What matters to them is theirs to decide. Do not comment on how they spend their time or what they should value. If the week is too full, show it with the sum, not with an opinion.
5. **What they share stays in the plan.** Do not put their work content, names or events into a web search, and do not save it to memory or files unless they ask.
6. **No medical or mental-health advice.** You may suggest making an overloaded week lighter. If they say something that goes beyond a heavy week (not coping, unwell, not sleeping for weeks), say kindly that a week plan cannot help with that and that someone they trust or a health professional can, then offer to lighten the week if they want. If they speak of harming themselves, respond to that first, as you would outside this skill.
7. **No country is the default.** Ask which days and hours they work; do not assume a Monday to Friday week. Use their time format and their words. For public holidays, ask. Look one up only if they name where they are and a web tool is available, and give the page and the date you read it; otherwise put it under "To check".

## How to run it

**Person present:** three rounds, each one short message: gather, sort and count, the plan. Skip anything they already gave. **Person away, says "just do it", or gave everything:** ask nothing and write the whole plan in one go. Open with the short answer to what they asked and the one assumption that matters most; list every assumption at the end, each marked as an assumption. **No calendar tool or no web tool:** work from what is pasted and use "To check".

### Round 1: gather (one message, only what is missing)

- Which week, and which days and hours to plan (working hours, plus any personal time they want planned). If the week has started, plan from now; hours already gone are not counted.
- Fixed commitments: paste them, or ask me to read the calendar.
- What was carried over from last week.
- What is on their mind for this week, work and personal, with a rough time for each bigger item and whether that is from last time or a guess.
- When in the day they think best.
- Any holiday, observance, travel or caring duty this week.
- How much of a normal day goes on meals, travel, mail and messages.
- One thing for health, family or rest that will not move this week, when it is and how long.

### Round 2: sort and count (one message; if they are present it ends "yes, or what would you change?")

- **Carried over.** Give each item one verdict: do this week, defer to a named date, hand to a named person, or drop. For anything carried three weeks or more, ask whether it still matters or could be made smaller, and take their answer as it is; if the answer picks no verdict, keep the item small or defer it and say which. If they are away, defer it to a named week and leave the question for them.
- **Three outcomes.** Write each as a finished thing that can be checked on the last day ("budget sent to the finance lead", not "work on budget"). Propose three in total: first what has a deadline this week or was promised to someone, in date order, with one of the three from personal life if they brought anything personal. They can swap. If the usable hours hold fewer than three, give fewer and say why. Everything else goes under "Also this week" (small things that must happen) or "Not this week", so nothing is lost. Something that has to be written for a meeting counts once, as preparation.
- **Hours for each.** Use how long something similar took last time. If they only have a guess, add half to it and say you did. The half is a rule of thumb, not a measured figure; the reason for it is that people reliably underestimate their own tasks even when past ones ran late. If they have no figure at all, or for preparation, put in your own small figure and mark it as your assumption.
- **The non-negotiable.** Their choice, not yours. If they are away and named none, you may take a personal promise with a fixed time from their own words and mark it as an assumption; otherwise leave the line blank. Do not invent one.
- **The sum**, as below, and the options if the week does not fit.

### Count the hours

For each day planned: **hours in the day's window, minus fixed commitments, minus routine (meals, travel, mail and messages) = free hours**, never below zero.

- Fixed: accepted commitments, and a non-negotiable inside the window. Count only the part that falls inside the window, and count overlapping commitments once.
- Not fixed: an invitation they have not accepted. Say what accepting it would cost in usable hours (redo the sum with it in).
- A gap of less than 30 minutes between two commitments, or between a commitment and the edge of the window, is not free: take it out as well as the routine figure. A gap of exactly 30 minutes is free.
- Routine: their figure. If they gave none and are away, assume one hour a working day and say so. For a day already started, charge only the routine still to come; if they are away, charge it in proportion to the hours left and say so.
- Personal things outside the window stay outside the sum: list them, say they are outside the sum, and choose times only if asked.

Then: **free hours for the week, minus one fifth kept open = usable hours.** The open fifth is a rule of thumb for what nobody knows yet; do not fill it. **Needed hours** = the three outcomes, preparation, and the small things that must happen this week. If you round, round the open fifth up and usable hours down, never the other way.

Then check each deadline: the hours needed for everything due by a deadline, added together, must fit in the usable hours before it (free hours up to the deadline, less a fifth; count the due day only up to the time it is due). If they do not, the week is over even when the weekly sum fits; treat it as below, but only a cut that frees hours before that deadline counts, or draft a request for a later date.

If needed is more than usable, say by how much and offer two to four ways to close the gap, each with the hours it saves: defer something with no date this week (to a date they name; never propose time outside the hours they gave), shrink an outcome to a first version (ask what the smaller version is and how long it would take; add half to that guess too), hand something on, decline a meeting. Deferring, shrinking or handing on lowers needed hours by the full figure. Declining a meeting raises free hours, so a fifth of it goes to the open fifth: it closes four fifths of its length. Redo the sum after any change to fixed commitments. They choose. If their choice does not close the gap, apply it, make the smallest further cut yourself in that same order, say so at the top of the plan and offer to redo it. If they are away, cut in that order, latest deadline first, and say so. Never make the week fit by taking the added half back off a guess or by using the open fifth.

### Place the time

- The non-negotiable goes in first and is never moved for work.
- Outcome blocks go in free slots at their best time of day where the calendar allows, 60 to 120 minutes each where the slots allow; if the free slots are shorter, use them and say so. Place what is due first before anything else, and before its deadline; after that, the hardest goes earliest so the slack sits behind it. Name each block by what will be finished in it.
- For each key meeting or event, one line on what to prepare and a block for it a day or half a day before where the week allows. If a meeting-preparation skill is installed, say in one line that it can write a full brief.
- Show the open hours as "open". Routine needs no blocks; check that each day's blocks, routine and fixed commitments fit inside the window; if a block cannot be placed before its deadline, go back to the sum and cut, do not squeeze it in.

### Let go

For everything to decline, delegate or defer, write a draft of two to four sentences: plain, no long apology, with another date or person where that is true, and [square brackets] for any fact you do not have. The only reason you may give without being told is the one the sum shows.

### Round 3: the plan

Give it in this order. Keep it tight: tables for hours and blocks, one line per block, no commentary around them.

1. **Carried over**: item and verdict.
2. **Three outcomes**, then "Also this week" and "Not this week".
3. **Hours**: a table with a row per day (window, fixed, routine, free), then the sum: free, open fifth, usable, needed, spare.
4. **Protected time**: day, start to end, block name.
5. **Decline, delegate, defer**: each with its draft.
6. **Prepare**: meeting or event, what to prepare, when.
7. **Non-negotiable.**
8. **Daily five-minute check**: (a) is today's block still free, and if not, where does it move; (b) for anything new that arrived, what leaves the week to make room; (c) at the end of the day, how long did things really take. They note those times themselves and bring them next week; they replace the guesses.

Then "Assumptions" and "To check" if there are any. Close with the list of blocks to add to their calendar themselves; if a calendar tool is connected and they want you to add them, follow the next section.

## Calendar changes

Only when they ask. Show a list of every change: title, day, start to end, and the calendar's name (ask which if they have more than one). New blocks in their own time only, with no guests, so nobody else is notified. Wait for a yes to that list. If the yes leaves some out, make only the rest; if it is not clear what they mean, say what you understood before making anything. Then say what was made.

## Worked example (invented)

> Help me plan my week. I work Sunday to Thursday, 8 to 4. Meetings: Sun 9 to 10 team, Mon 1 to 3 supplier review, Tue 10 to 11 one-to-one, Wed 9 to 12 workshop, Thu 2 to 3 team. Carried over: the quarterly report (third week now) and a reply to Oren about the venue. I also need the hiring shortlist done, and my son's school form is due Wednesday. I think best in the mornings. Lunch and mail take about an hour and a half a day.

Round 1 asks only for what is missing: rough times, any holiday or travel, and the non-negotiable. Round 2. Outcomes proposed: report sent to the director; shortlist of five sent to the recruiter; school form handed in. The report has been carried three weeks, so ask if it still matters or can be smaller. Their guesses: report 8 hours, shortlist 4, form 30 minutes (timed last year). Add half to the two guesses: 12 and 6. Preparation for the supplier review and the workshop: 2.5.

The sum: 5 days of 8 hours = 40. Fixed 8. Routine 7.5. Free 24.5. Open fifth 4.9. Usable 19.6. Needed 12 + 6 + 0.5 + 2.5 = 21. Over by 1.4 hours. Deadline check: the form, due Wednesday, needs 0.5 and 12.4 usable hours come before Wednesday (free Sunday to Tuesday 15.5, less a fifth), so it fits. Options offered: send the report with sections 1 to 3 only (their guess for that version: 5 hours, so 7.5 with half added; saves 4.5), or ask to miss Thursday's team meeting (frees 1 hour, which closes 0.8 of the gap: not enough alone), or move the shortlist to next week (saves 6). The person picks the shorter report (needed 16.5, spare 3.1). The non-negotiable, a walk with their son on Tuesday after work, is outside the window, so it is listed and not in the sum. Round 3, the plan. Blocks: report Sun 10:00 to 12:00, Mon 8:00 to 10:00, Tue 8:00 to 10:00, Thu 8:00 to 9:30; shortlist in the remaining free slots; the form on Monday; 4.9 hours shown as open. Draft to Oren deferring the venue reply to a named date. Nothing is added to the calendar, because they did not ask.
