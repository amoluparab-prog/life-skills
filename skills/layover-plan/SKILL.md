# Layover plan: what a wait between connections is really good for

A nine-hour gap is never nine hours. Getting off, border checks, the ride each way, security and a margin eat most of it, and guessing wrong means a missed flight. This skill shows the sums, gives one verdict, and sets a turn-back time that does not move.

## Rules that always hold

1. **Entry and transit rules never come from memory.** State one only if you read it this session on a government, airline or airport site, with the source and date. Otherwise write "to confirm" and say who to ask. This covers well-known rules too; they change, and they differ by passport, ticket and bags.
2. **Never cut it fine.** Always state the margin and what a miss would cost. If the sums are tight, the verdict is to stay.
3. **Unknown is said as unknown.** No one publishes tomorrow's queue. Use the allowance below and label it an allowance.
4. **Border rules are kept.** Never suggest skipping a check, misstating the purpose of a visit, or leaving when entry is not allowed.
5. **People before sights.** Children, limited mobility, tiredness and night-time shrink the plan first.
6. **The traveller books and pays.** Book nothing, contact no one. Ask only for passport and visa status (nationality, visas or permits held), never a number.

## Step 1: get the facts (ask once)

Use what the message gives. Ask for the rest in one short message, most important first:

- Where (airport or station, and terminals if known), arrival and departure times and date, and the airline or operator with flight or train numbers.
- Arriving from and leaving for where (domestic or international on each side).
- One ticket or separate tickets. Checked bags tagged through, or to be collected.
- Passport nationality and any visas or residence permits held, as the traveller states them.
- Who is travelling (children, anyone who walks slowly or needs assistance; both count as limited mobility below), how tired they will be, budget, interests.

If they cannot answer or are away, go on with stated assumptions and give the plan as "if this, then that". If the gap is under 2 hours, say stay, say how to find the way to the gate (signs, transfer desk, airport map), and stop. If it is over about 24 hours it is a short visit; say so and plan only the return to the airport. An unplanned wait after a missed or cancelled connection is in scope once the new flight is settled.

## Step 2: look up what can be looked up

Keep it to a handful of pages, in this order, and date each fact: entry and transit, airline, the ride, then facilities. Blogs and general knowledge can suggest sights and food in the city, with hours and prices marked "not checked"; they never settle a rule.

- **Government site of the layover country** (and the traveller's own government's travel advice): may this passport enter, is a visa or travel authorisation needed to enter or even to stay airside, must every arriving passenger clear the border. If only the traveller's own government's advice was read, it counts as confirmed, but say so and keep the layover country's own site or the airline's document check on the confirm list. If pages allow, check the onward country's entry rule for this passport too; otherwise put it on the confirm list. If the main page cannot be read, another site of the same government (its embassy or foreign ministry) will do.
- **Airline**: are bags tagged through, when check-in and bag drop open and close, when the gate closes, later flights that day, what happens after a missed connection on this kind of ticket.
- **Airport or station**: how transfers work, terminal-to-terminal time, security hours, whether the airside area or terminal closes at night, rest areas, showers, lounges that sell entry, hotels inside, food, quiet rooms, play areas, left luggage (place, hours, price), and any tour the airport itself runs for transit passengers, with its conditions.
- **Transit operator**: journey time, how often, first and last service, fare, how to pay.

If you have no web access, or a page is refused, do not retry and do not fill the gap from memory. Use the allowances, mark each item "to confirm" with where to check it, and make any plan outside the terminal conditional. Never give a lounge, shop or facility inside the airport by name unless you read it on a page this session; say what kind of thing to look for and where to check.

## Step 3: do the sums

Work in minutes, in the local time of the layover place, counting back from **when the gate closes**, not from departure. Show a table: step, minutes, source or "allowance". Use a looked-up figure when you have one; otherwise these:

| Step | Allowance |
|---|---|
| Off the plane or train and to the exit | 30 (45 at a very large airport) |
| Entry checks on arrival | 90 international, 0 domestic |
| Collect and store bags, if not tagged through | 30 |
| Ride out and ride back | journey time at a busy hour, plus one wait for the next service, each way; if no figure can be found, 60 each way to the city or 20 to somewhere near, labelled a guess |
| Bag drop, if bags must be checked again | 30 for the queue; then check the plan reaches the desk before the airline's cut-off (60 before departure if unknown) |
| Security and exit checks on return | 60 international, 30 domestic |
| Walk to the gate | 20 (add 15 here and to the first line for limited mobility; add the airport's figure for a change of terminal, else 30) |
| Gate closes before departure | airline's figure, else 30 |
| Safety margin | 60; 90 with separate tickets, children, limited mobility, road traffic, night, or no later flight that day (unknown counts as none) |

Free time = gap minus every line. Then give clock times: out of the terminal, **latest time to set out** (the out-of-terminal time plus free time minus the verdict's minimum; after it, drop to the next verdict down), **turn-back time** (leave wherever you are), back at the terminal door, at the gate. For a stay verdict give instead: landing, through any transfer checks (60 if unknown, to confirm), an alarm to start walking, at the gate 20 minutes before it closes, gate closes. Check that the steps add back up to the gap; run the sums in code if you can. A late arrival comes off the free time minute for minute; the turn-back time never moves. For a station, drop the border and security lines unless the route has them.

## Step 4: give one verdict

Blockers first. If entry is not confirmed from an official source, or bags or tickets force a step that needs it, the verdict is **stay airside**, with the outside plan shown as "only if X confirms". Staying airside can itself need a transit visa. Any unconfirmed item that could stop the journey itself, or that must be arranged before flying, goes at the top of the reply, above the plan, with who settles it and how soon. If the sums fail as well, give no outside plan; say what gap would have been needed.

With bags to check again, or separate tickets and no onward boarding pass yet, the traveller usually cannot go airside until the onward airline's desk opens, so "stay" means inside the terminal on the public side until then. Look up or flag the desk opening time, build the plan around it, and if it leaves less than the full margin say so plainly and say what helps (online check-in, being first in the queue).

Then by free time at the place itself, worked out with the ride to that place (city or near):

- **Go into the city**: 120 minutes or more.
- **Leave the terminal but stay near** (within about 20 minutes): 45 to 120 minutes.
- **Stay airside**: less than 45.

Go one step down for each of these that applies, stopping at stay: they say they will be tired, have small children, or it is night. Say the cost of a miss in plain words: who pays for a new ticket (on separate tickets, normally the traveller, unless the seller guaranteed the connection), whether the rest of the ticket can be cancelled, where the checked bags go, and when the next flight is, looked up or marked unknown.

## Step 5: write the reply

1. **Verdict** in one or two lines, with the turn-back time and any condition.
2. **The sums** as the table, then the clock times.
3. **Plans**: the stay plan always (rest, shower, food, a walk; for an overnight wait, where to sleep, comparing a room inside or beside the terminal with resting in the terminal; with where and what it costs in local money when you read it, otherwise the kind of thing to look for), and the outside plan if it qualifies. Each as clock times, fitted to energy, budget and interests, with one thing to drop first if time runs short.
4. **Bags, transit, money**: left luggage, the ride, tickets and how to pay, cash or card, phone data.
5. **Confirm before you leave**: each open item and who settles it. Always include entry and transit permission, bags, the onward boarding pass and gate closing time; add security and terminal hours when any of the wait falls at night.
6. **Staying safe**: two alarms (turn-back, gate), passport and boarding pass on you, phone charged, clock on local time, and for night, children or mobility the points that apply (booked assistance, lit and staffed places, last service).

Leave out lines that do not apply, and keep each section to what this traveller will use; the reply gets read on a phone in a queue, so aim for under about 800 words. If the user could not answer, end with the two or three questions that would change the plan most.

## Worked example

"9 h 30 at Exampleport, land 06:10 from abroad, leave 15:40 for abroad, same terminal. One ticket, bags through. Passport from Country A, no visas. Rested and curious, small budget."

Looked up: train to the centre 25 minutes every 10 (operator site); gate closes 30 minutes before, and a later flight the same evening (airline). Not found: whether a Country A passport may enter.

| Step | Min | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Gap 06:10 to 15:40 | 570 | ticket |
| Off the plane | 30 | allowance |
| Entry checks | 90 | allowance, queue unknown |
| Train out, with wait | 35 | operator site |
| Train back, with wait | 35 | operator site |
| Security and exit checks | 60 | allowance, queue unknown |
| Walk to gate | 20 | allowance |
| Gate closes early | 30 | airline site |
| Safety margin | 60 | allowance; a later flight exists |
| **Free in the city** | **210** | |

Verdict: "Stay airside unless Exampleland's immigration site confirms your passport can enter without a visa. If it does, go into the city, and leave it by 12:15 whatever happens." Clock times: out of the terminal about 08:10, in the centre 08:45, turn-back 12:15, terminal door 12:50, gate 14:10, gate closes 15:10. Latest time to set out: 09:40 (08:10 plus 210 minus 120); after that, stay near or stay in. Cost of a miss: the next flight is this evening, and if the miss is your own doing you may pay a new fare; to confirm with the airline. Then the airside plan, the basics and the confirm list follow.
