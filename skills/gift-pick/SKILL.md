# Gift pick: choose a gift this person will value

Givers tend to picture the moment the gift is opened. Receivers live with it afterwards. This skill pulls the choice back to the receiver's side, then checks every idea against the real world: price, stock, delivery, customs.

## Rules that hold throughout

- **Start from the person.** Every idea must tie to something the giver told you about the recipient. If an idea would suit anyone, drop it.
- **Nothing from memory about prices, stock or delivery.** Each carries where it was seen and the date. Never say a gift will arrive in time; quote the seller's stated delivery date or window and compare it with the deadline. If the seller gives none, say so.
- **No steering.** No affiliate, referral or tracking links, and no favourite shop. Name the seller as "seen at", add a second seller when you find one, and say any seller will do. A plain link to a page you read is fine.
- **Customs come with their source and a caveat.** Say who describes the custom, and that families and individuals differ. The giver knows the family; you do not.
- **Nothing that comments on the recipient's body, age, weight or health** (diet and fitness items, anti-ageing products, hair-loss or mobility aids, "over the hill" jokes, self-improvement books) unless the giver says the recipient asked for it.
- **Ask before suggesting, do not assume:** alcohol, scent, clothing that needs a size, pets or live animals, anything for the recipient's home that needs space or upkeep. (This list is this skill's own caution.) If the giver cannot be asked, include such an idea only when their own words point to it (the recipient complained about that very thing), and put the open question under "To check".
- **When the giver raises an item from the two lists above** (their own idea or a relative's), answer it plainly and kindly: what you could check, what only the recipient or family can say, and whether you would give it. Keep it conditional on that answer; do not lecture.
- **Do not look the recipient up online** by name or account. Work from what the giver says.
- **Never buy, reserve or add to a basket.** The giver decides and buys.

## 1. Ask once, in one short batch

Skip what the request already answers. Ask the rest in one message, and say they can answer in a few words:

1. Who it is for: relationship, rough age band, what they do and enjoy.
2. What they have said they want, own already, or dislike. Is there a wish list or registry?
3. The occasion and its date, and any cultural, religious or family customs you should know about.
4. Where the recipient is (city and country), where you will buy it, and how it gets there: handed over, posted, or carried in luggage.
5. Budget, with currency, and whether others might chip in.
6. What you have given them before, and how it went.

Ask one follow-up only if an answer changes the whole answer (a child's exact age, a workplace limit). Then go on.

**If the user is away, says "just give me ideas", or cannot reply:** do not ask and do not end on a question. Write the full answer on stated assumptions, list them at the top, and put what you could not know under "To check before you buy".

## 2. Read the person

In three or four lines, say which one or two kinds of gift fit, and why, using the giver's own words about the recipient:

- **Useful:** something they will use often, a better version of a thing they already reach for.
- **Experience:** something to do: a class, a ticket, a day out.
- **Consumable:** used up and gone: food, drink, flowers, supplies for a hobby. Good when they "have everything" or little space.
- **Sentimental:** carries a memory or a tie between you: a photo, a letter, something restored.
- **Shared:** time or an activity with the giver or the household.

Lean on what research on gifts has found (Galak, Givi and Williams 2016, and the studies it reviews; Chan and Mogilner 2016). These are averages across study participants, not laws, and what the recipient has said always wins. In the reply, say "research on gifts" in plain words; name the papers only if asked:

- If they asked for something, give that. Asked-for gifts were appreciated more than surprises and seen as more thoughtful. One named wish outranks a clever idea of yours.
- Favour what they will use over what looks impressive when opened. Easy to use beats grand but awkward (a far-off venue, a fixed date, a thing that needs assembly or upkeep).
- Spending more does not buy more appreciation. Never push the budget up; a cheaper, better-fitting idea is a win.
- Experiences tend to bring people closer than objects, and so can an object that stirs a memory. Make an experience easy to redeem.
- A donation in their name is appreciated less than givers expect; keep it for someone who asked or whose cause you know.
- Money or a general gift card is often welcomed more than givers expect; whether it is proper here is a customs question (section 4).

## 3. Guard rails for three cases

- **A child.** The parent's or guardian's wishes come first: ask the giver to check with them (allowed items, screens, noise, sweets, size, what the child already has). Check the maker's age label on each idea; an under-3 warning is a safety warning, not a skill rating. Avoid small parts for under-threes, small strong magnets and unsecured button batteries; add a helmet to anything ridden. Look up which toy safety mark applies in the recipient's country and tell the giver to look for it on the product and to check that country's recall list.
- **Work (boss, colleague, client, teacher, official).** Ask whether the workplace or the recipient's organisation has a gift policy or value limit, and stay inside it. If unknown, say "check the policy first", keep it modest and not personal, prefer something shareable or a group gift, and never cash unless local custom and the policy both allow it. A gift upward to a boss is best small or from the group.
- **A sensitive time (illness, loss, a hard year).** Keep it gentle; comfort and company over cheer or fixing.

## 4. Look it up

1. **Customs first**, because they remove ideas. Search for gift customs for this occasion in the recipient's culture and country: what is usual, what is avoided (items, numbers, colours, wrapping), whether money is proper, how a gift is handed over and whether it is opened then. Prefer a cultural reference work, a national or community organisation, or the tradition's own sites over a shop's blog. Read the page, not the snippet.
2. **Then ideas.** For each candidate, find a real example on sale where the giver will buy it (usually a seller that delivers inside the recipient's country; the giver's own town when the gift is carried): seller, price in the seller's currency, date seen, the seller's stated delivery time to that place, and for an experience how it is redeemed and when it expires. Read the seller's page. A handful of lookups per idea at most; if lookups are short, spend them on the likely safe pick first and still give five ideas, the unchecked ones marked.
3. If a page cannot be opened or you did not look, keep the idea and mark the line "to check: price and delivery not confirmed". A search snippet's price is "seen in a search result, not confirmed". If the one listing you opened looks weak (poor seller rating, no details), say so and treat it as proof the thing can be had at about that price, not as the one to buy.
4. **Budget in another currency:** look up the exchange rate and give it with source and date, or mark your conversion "a rough guess, check the rate".
5. **Carried or posted across a border:** look up the destination country's official rules for bringing in that kind of thing (food, plants, alcohol, batteries) and any import charge, or mark it "to check". A toy bought in one country may not carry the recipient country's safety mark; check the packaging and rely on the maker's age label.
6. **When a customs source is silent** on this occasion or item, say that it is silent. Anything you add from memory, here or elsewhere (a venue, a rule), is labelled "from memory, not checked".

**No search tool, or nothing opens:** say so at the top. Still give the read, the avoid list and the card message. Give the ideas as kinds of thing described closely enough to search for ("a hand-bound A5 sketchbook, plain heavy paper"), with "seen at: not looked up" plus words to search for, no brand, price or delivery claim, and at most a rough "usually within / usually above your budget" marked as a guess. Give customs, safety marks, border rules and any place or venue you name only as "from memory, not checked", and turn each guard-rail lookup into a line for the giver to check. End with a "To check before you buy" list: what to search for, what to confirm with the seller, what to ask the family.

## 5. Write the answer, in this order

1. **Assumptions** (only if you made any), one or two lines. For a hurried giver, one line with your pick may go above them.
2. **The read.** Section 2's three or four lines. If the giver proposed ideas of their own, assess each here, briefly, before yours.
3. **Five or six ideas**, each a separate thing (a bundle of two of them or a free gesture is an extra, not one of the five), from well under the budget up to it, never above unless the giver said others may chip in. Do not pad to reach the budget. At least two different kinds. For a household with one budget, ideas may be parts of one combined gift: give the combined total against the budget, or say which prices are missing. For each idea, four short lines:
   - what it is, and **why it fits this person** (tie to something they said);
   - **seen at** seller, price, date; a second seller if found;
   - **delivery:** the seller's stated time against the deadline, as "fits", "tight" or "does not fit", or "to check";
   - **make it personal:** one small, concrete touch (a note about why, an inscription, pairing it with a plan to use it together).
4. **Safe pick and bolder pick**, one line each with the reason. Safe: lowest chance of missing. Bolder: could delight, with what makes it a risk.
5. **Avoid** for this occasion or culture: each item with its source and "families differ; you know them best".
6. **Card message:** two to four sentences in the giver's voice, naming something true about the recipient, with no joke about age or body. Offer one alternative tone only if asked.
7. **To check before you buy:** sizes, the parent's or policy's yes, stock on the day, the return or exchange terms, anything marked "to check".

If the deadline is too close for delivery, say so plainly and move ideas to ones that can be bought locally, collected, or given as a booking with a hand-written note.

**Later turns.** The same rules hold for every follow-up: a new idea from the giver gets a looked-up or "not checked" answer, the guard rails apply again, and a request to order is declined with the details they need to buy it themselves.

## When not to use this

Something for yourself or your own home (a big-purchase skill); planning the party, menu or guest list; a speech or toast; corporate, bulk or branded gifts and client gift programmes; setting up a registry or a year-round gift roster. Say it is outside this skill and answer normally or hand off. In a mixed request (a party plan plus a present), help with the present and hand off the rest.

## Example

(Names, sellers and prices are invented to show the shape; look everything up again.)

User: "Help me pick an anniversary gift, our tenth, on 14 March. She paints watercolours on weekends, loves the coast, hates clutter, and said last month her travel brush set is falling apart. Budget about 150 euros, we live in Porto. Last year I gave earrings she never wears."

Reply, in outline. The read: useful plus shared. She named a need, which outranks a surprise; she dislikes clutter, so nothing for a shelf; the earrings suggest guessing her taste is risky. Ideas: (1) a travel brush set like the one wearing out: "seen at Loja Aguarela Exemplo, 48 euros, on 2 March; also at Papelaria Exemplo, 52 euros; delivery stated 2 to 3 working days to Porto: fits; make it personal: a note saying where you hope she paints next". (2) A block of heavy cotton paper, seen at Papelaria Exemplo, 22 euros, on 2 March; delivery to check. (3) A one-day coastal painting workshop for two, seen at Atelier Costa Exemplo, 120 euros, on 2 March, open date, voucher by email: fits. (4) A night on the coast with her paints packed, to check: price for that weekend. (5) Her favourite painting of the coast professionally framed, to check: the framer's price and lead time. Safe pick: the brush set, because she asked. Bolder pick: the workshop for two; the risk is that she paints to be alone. Avoid: another piece of jewellery chosen by guess; a "tin" theme for a tenth anniversary is described by the Guia de Etiqueta Exemplo (seen 2 March); no need to follow it; families differ, and you know her best. Card: "Ten years, and I still like watching you look at the sea before you paint it. These are for the next ten." To check: brush sizes she uses; workshop dates.
