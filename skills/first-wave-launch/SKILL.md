# First-wave launch

The first users of a new thing rarely arrive from an announcement. They come because someone asked them personally, one at a time, and then made sure it worked for them. This skill turns the reach a person already has into one week of that work.

## Ground rules (they hold even if the person asks otherwise)

- **Draft only.** The person sends every message and post from their own accounts. Do not send, post, schedule or automate anything, even if a tool would let you.
- **No bulk or automated messaging**, no scraped contact lists, no bought lists, no fake or extra accounts, no fake reviews, testimonials, votes or sign-ups. These get accounts banned, can break the law, and bring users who never return. If asked for one, say no in a line and offer the by-hand version.
- **Each community's own rules win.** Where a group forbids promotion, say so plainly and leave it out of the plan.
- **Never invent numbers.** No made-up user counts, quotes, traction or reply rates. Counts come from the person; a missing one is written as a blank such as `[your number]`.
- **Honest about today.** Describe what the product does now, not what is planned.

## 1. Get the facts

Take what the request already gives. If something below is missing, ask for it once, in one short message, and say they can answer "not sure" to any of it. One follow-up question is allowed if the audience is still too wide to reach or the action is still open; after that, go ahead with blanks.

1. What it is, the link, and what works today.
2. Who it is for. Push for a narrow group ("runners training for a first 10k in Pune", not "people who like fitness"); a small group you can mostly reach beats a wide one you cannot.
3. The one action you want from a first user (sign up, join, post once, come on the day). If they are torn, recommend the one that shows real use and say why.
4. Reach, with rough numbers per channel: people they could message personally, groups and communities they are already a member of, followers or subscribers.
5. Launch date, and how much time per day they can give.
6. If email to strangers is likely: which countries those people are in.

If the person is away or says "just draft it", go ahead: use what you have, mark every gap as a blank, and list the assumptions at the top. Either way, end the plan with one short list of the blanks still to fill.

## 2. Build the plan

Give all seven parts in one reply, in this order, under plain headings.

**Offer.** One line a stranger would understand: "For [who], [thing] that [result]." Then the single action and the link. One action only; a second ask competes with the first.

**Rings.** Work from the inside out, because people who know the person are willing to try it and easy to ask what broke, and that fixes the thing before strangers see it.

| Ring | Who | How they are asked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | People who know them | One personal message each |
| 2 | Communities they already belong to, and their own followers or subscribers | A post or a mention as a member, where the rules allow; one post on their own feed |
| 3 | Strangers who fit | Introductions, public places that welcome it, one reply per thread to people who asked for such a thing, written for that thread (only where that community allows links) |

A large list of loose connections or old contacts is not ring 1: the ones they really know go in ring 1; the rest are not messaged, and hear of it only through a post they would see anyway. Contact details that came from an employer's or client's customers or systems are left out altogether.

For each ring give the count to **ask**, taken from their numbers. In ring 1 everyone can test it and say what broke; add a blank for how many of them fit the "who it is for" line, since only the person knows. For ring 2 the count is the number of posts, with each audience's size beside it; ring 3 is a blank until places or people are named. Do not forecast how many will reply or sign up. If they have a target, show the sum only with their own rate from the first two days ("12 of 30 tried it, so 100 users needs about 250 asks at this rate"), and say it is a floor, not a forecast: people who do not know them will answer less often.

**Channel rules.** Before drafting for a named community, read its rules (sidebar, pinned post, about page) if you have a web tool, and mark each channel: allowed, ask a moderator first, forbidden, or not checked. With no web tool, or if the page will not open (closed and private groups never will), mark it "not checked" and ask the person to read or paste the rules. For "ask a moderator first", draft that question. Then apply these:
- Posts: say "I built this", post once per community, never ask for votes, and take part as a member rather than only promoting.
- Messaging apps such as WhatsApp: one chat at a time, to people who know the sender. No bulk-send tools, and do not add anyone to a group without asking them. Stop when someone asks.
- Email to strangers, anywhere: keep it rare and individual. The message must show who is writing, have an honest subject, and give a simple way to say "do not contact me again". An exported list of connections or contacts is not a list of people who agreed to email; one message to all of them is a bulk mailing, so decline it. Whenever email rules come up, say this is general information, not legal advice.
- Recipients in the US: a single promotional email is covered by the same law as a mass mailing. It must also say it is promotional and carry a postal address (a registered PO box counts, so nobody needs to publish a home address), and a request to stop must be honoured.
- Recipients in the UK: marketing email or text to a private person, sole traders included, generally needs their consent first, so do not draft cold email to individuals there; use public places or an introduction. Treat every other country, and any recipient whose country is unknown, like the UK unless the person tells you its rules allow it.

**Launch week.** Say which day is day 1. If there is a fixed date when the thing first exists (a first issue, an event, an opening), the week leads up to it. With more than a week in hand, run it early enough to give people notice and add one reminder to those who said yes; with less, shorten ring 1 to a day. With no fixed date it starts whenever they do. If places are limited, say the true number and stop going wider when it is reached. A row per day: which ring, how many messages (about 10 to 20 personal ones a day is plenty for one person; fit it to their time; if several people are sending, that is per person and each writes to the people they know), and a slot to reply and fix. A usual shape:
- Before day 1: do the action yourself as a new user would; set up the tracker.
- Days 1 and 2: ring 1. Fix what they trip over the same day.
- Days 3 and 4: ring 2, one community a day so each thread can be answered.
- Days 5 and 6: ring 3 (if it is still a blank, ask each ring 1 person who tried it for one introduction), plus one polite nudge to ring 1 people who did not answer. One nudge only.
- Day 7: count, decide (see Measure), thank everyone who tried it.

**Messages.** One short draft per ring and channel in use. Each has: a personal opening line left as a blank for the person to write (`[how you know them / why them]`), one line on what it is, the one action, and an easy way to say no. A few sentences at most. No pressure, no "limited spots" unless true. Tell the person to change each one before sending; identical pasted messages read as spam and are treated as spam.

**Tracker.** A table with columns: Name, Ring, Channel, Asked (date), Replied, Tried it, Came back, What they said, Follow-up. Make it a spreadsheet or CSV file if you can create files; otherwise a table in the reply. The person fills in names; do not pull them from anywhere.

**Measure, and when to change course.** Count four things each day: asked, replied, tried it, came back. Say what "tried it" and "came back" mean for this thing (for a newsletter: signed up, then opened the second issue). If nothing can be used until after the week, say that "came back" and the feedback question wait until it can. Have the person set the line before launch; offer these as rules of thumb, not benchmarks:
- Ring 1 mostly does not try it: the offer or the action is unclear. Fix that before going wider.
- People try it and nobody comes back or finishes: the problem is the product, not the reach. Pause outreach and talk to five of them.
- One kind of person is clearly keener than the rest: narrow to them and find more like them.

**Feedback question.** Ask everyone who tried it, in a personal message: "What nearly stopped you, or what was confusing?" For people who came back, add: "How would you feel if you could no longer use this?" With a few dozen users the answers are a signal, not a score. Never ask "do you like it?"; people say yes to be kind.

## Example

"I built a free site that matches people in my city for weekend hikes. Launching Saturday. I have about 60 hiking friends on WhatsApp, I'm in two local Facebook hiking groups (4,000 and 900 members), and 300 Instagram followers. An hour a day."

The reply gives: offer "For people in [city] who want company on weekend hikes: pick a date and get matched with a small group", action "join one hike in the next two weekends". Ring 1: all 60 friends, asked one chat at a time, 15 a day on days 1 to 4 (an hour a day will not cover 60 in two days, so ring 2 moves to days 5 and 6), with `[your number]` for how many live in the city and hike. Ring 2: both groups marked "not checked" until their rules are read; if one bans promotion it is dropped, and the other gets one post saying "I built this"; plus one post to the 300 Instagram followers. Ring 3: `[your number]` until places are named. Then the remaining parts, with no forecast of sign-ups.

## Not for

A job search (use a job-search skill), paid ads, writing or building the product, a press release, pricing, a pitch to a decision-maker, or long-term growth and SEO. Say so and point to the better fit. If a request mixes one of these with finding first users, do the first-users part and say the rest is outside this skill.
