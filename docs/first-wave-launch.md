# first-wave-launch

Status: **tested and reviewed, with one open item**. Two test rounds and two independent reviews were run; every line scored 4 or 5 at the final review. The edits made after the second test round, including every fix the reviews asked for, have not been run by a test session. Version 0.1.0.

## What it does

Helps a solo builder or a small team get the first wave of real users for something they are launching: a site, an app, a community, a newsletter or an event. From what it is, who it is for and the reach the person already has, it writes a one-week plan:

1. A one-line offer and the single action wanted from a first user.
2. The audience in three rings: people who know them, communities they already belong to (plus their own followers), and strangers who fit. Each ring has a count to ask, taken from the person's own numbers.
3. The rules of each channel: allowed, ask a moderator first, forbidden, or not checked.
4. A day-by-day launch week sized to the time they have.
5. Short message drafts per ring and channel, each with a blank first line for the person to make it personal.
6. A tracker: who was asked, replied, tried it, came back.
7. What to count in week one, what result means change course, and one feedback question for every early user.

Rules it keeps even when asked otherwise: it drafts only, and the person sends everything by hand; no bulk or automated messaging, scraped or bought lists, fake accounts, reviews or testimonials; each community's own rules win, and a channel that forbids promotion is left out; no invented user numbers, quotes, reply rates or forecasts; honest about what the product does today.

Its notes on email rules are general information, not legal advice.

## How it differs from similar public skills

Several public skills cover launches and early growth. These were seen on skill directory pages (their full text was not read): "launch-strategy" (in a few repositories, for example alirezarezvani/claude-skills), which plans a product or feature launch across owned, rented and borrowed channels; "cold-start-strategy" (kostja94/marketing-skills), a multi-week, multi-channel playbook for software tools; "first-1000-users" (openclaw/skills), a Reddit agent that drafts replies and posts approved ones through the Reddit API; and "gtm-0-to-1-launch" and "ph-community-outreach". This skill is narrower on purpose: the first week only, built from the reach the person already has, in messages they send one at a time by hand. It never posts, automates or scrapes, checks each community's rules first, gives a per-person tracker and a stated change-course rule, and works for launches that are not software. Nothing was copied from those skills.

## Example

> I built a free site that matches people in my city for weekend hikes. Launching Saturday. I have about 60 hiking friends on WhatsApp, I'm in two local Facebook hiking groups (4,000 and 900 members), and 300 Instagram followers. An hour a day.

The reply, in outline. Offer: "For people in [city] who want company on weekend hikes: pick a date and get matched with a small group." Action: join one hike in the next two weekends. Ring 1: all 60 friends, one chat at a time, 15 a day, with a blank for how many live in the city and hike. Ring 2: both groups marked "not checked" until their rules are read (one that bans promotion is dropped; the other gets a single post saying "I built this"), plus one post to the 300 followers. Ring 3: a blank until places are named. Then the week, the drafts, for example:

> [how you know them / why them]
> I've made a free site that matches people here for weekend hikes: you pick a date and get put in a small group. Would you try joining one in the next two weekends and tell me what was confusing? [link]
> No worries at all if not.

and the tracker, the change-course lines and the feedback question ("What nearly stopped you, or what was confusing?"). No forecast of sign-ups.

## Not for

A job search or outreach to employers; paid advertising plans; building the product; a press release; pricing or paywall design; a pitch to a decision-maker or investor; long-term growth and SEO.

What the sources support: recruiting first users by hand, starting with a narrow group and finding more of the keenest users (Graham); using few channels, where the users already are (Rachitsky); the "how would you feel if you could no longer use this" question, used here as a signal and without its 40% line (Ellis); that US law covers a single promotional email and what it must carry (FTC); that UK marketing email or text to individuals generally needs consent (ICO); that bulk and automated messaging can cost a WhatsApp account (WhatsApp).

Not from a source (the author's judgement): the three rings and the inside-out order, about 10 to 20 personal messages a day, one nudge only, the change-course rules of thumb, the wording of the first feedback question, not adding people to groups without asking, treating every country other than the US and UK as consent-first, and the lines "a second ask competes with the first", "identical pasted messages read as spam" and "bring users who never return". The advice not to ask "do you like it?" is general know-how, often associated with Rob Fitzpatrick's book The Mom Test, which was not re-read for this skill. No EU or other country's email law was read.
