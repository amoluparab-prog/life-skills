# ritual-guide

Status: tested and reviewed (two test rounds, two independent reviews, every rubric line at 4 or above), with two limits stated plainly: no test produced the written guide with source pages read and a looked-up local time, and the wording edits made after the second test round have not been run. See the testing record. Version 0.1.0.

## What it does

Helps a person prepare and carry out a religious or cultural observance of their own tradition at home: a festival puja, a Seder, a housewarming blessing, a memorial observance, a fast-breaking meal and similar. It is made for first-time hosts and people far from family.

The skill holds no ritual content of its own. It looks each observance up when asked, prefers the tradition's own recognised bodies and texts (the person's own stream first), and reports what named sources say.

1. Asks once, in one short batch: the observance, the tradition and community, the place and date, who will be there, what they have, and whether they want one written guide or a live walk-through.
2. Looks it up and reads the pages.
3. Writes six parts: what it is; what to gather, with ordinary substitutes; when, with how the date or window is set and where to confirm it locally; the order of steps; what happens afterwards; and "must I" answers, each labelled Required per a named source, Common custom, Varies, or Not confirmed.
4. Or walks the person through it live: one "before we start" message, then one step per message, waiting each time.
5. Works fixed safety lines into the plan for flame, food for a group and fasting.

Rules it keeps throughout: every tradition gets the same respect, with no ranking, judging or arguing of belief; nothing is invented, and no prayer wording is written from memory; every requirement carries its source and the stream that source speaks for; the person's own elder, priest, rabbi, imam or community has the final word; dates and times are looked up, never worked out; rites that need an officiant are not rewritten as do-it-yourself; no medical advice; no long copyrighted translations.

When nothing can be looked up (no search tool, or pages cannot be opened), it says so at the top and gives a shorter guide marked "from general knowledge, not checked", with no dates, no clock times and no sacred wording. A reply of that kind is a starting list of things to confirm, not a checked guide.

## How it differs from similar public skills

A search on 21 November 2026 found nothing that does this job. The nearest were a "tradition observance calendar" skill by OKHP3 (year calendars of Jewish, Christian and Islamic holidays; dates only), "shabbat-aware-scheduler" by skills-il (schedules work around Shabbat and Israeli holidays, and leaves religious rulings to a rabbi), and a "personal-ritual-archive" listing with no description. ritual-guide is about one home observance, prepared and carried out, with sourced labels and safety. Nothing was copied from those skills.

## Example

> What do I need for a Seder and what is the order? First time hosting, six adults, in Chicago. We're Reform, and two guests have never been to one.

The reply, in outline: a top line saying it was looked up that day, with sources named. What a Seder is. A gather list sized for six. When: the rule as sources give it (after nightfall on the first night), the date and time for Chicago from a Jewish calendar source with the day it was looked up, and "confirm with your congregation". The named steps in order, noting that one source counts 14 and another 15 for the same order, with the Haggadah as the place the words are found. What happens afterwards. "Must I" answers with labels, for example "A second night? Varies: both sources describe two nights outside Israel; ask your congregation." A note that neither source read was Reform. Candle and food safety lines. A closing line: families differ, and your rabbi or congregation is the authority.

## Not for

Comparing or debating religions; essays or research about a tradition; planning a wedding or large public event; horoscopes or choosing a personal auspicious date; what to wear (event-outfit in this collection); a guest asking how to behave at someone else's observance; general party planning.
