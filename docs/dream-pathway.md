# dream-pathway

Status: tested and reviewed, version 0.1.0. Two test rounds and two independent reviews were run; every rubric line scored 4 or 5 at the final review. Open: the wording changes made after the last test round have not been run (see the testing record).

## What it does

Helps a parent, guardian or mentor turn a young person's big goal (a sport, a performing art, a field of study, a craft) into a realistic route. It asks for the goal in the young person's own words, an age band, their current level, the time and money available and the region, then writes one plan:

1. The goal and where they are now, with any assumptions marked.
2. Three to five honest phases from here to the goal, what each requires, and the odds in plain words. No figure unless one was found in a named source.
3. An opportunity calendar (trials, auditions, competitions, programmes, scholarships, deadlines) looked up from official sources at the time, each with its source, the date checked and a scam-screen result. Anything not confirmed is marked "to check"; nothing is invented.
4. Who could help, and a short message for the parent to send through an official channel.
5. A weekly routine that puts school first and protects a day off, sleep and fun.
6. A simple tracker that includes how much they enjoyed it.
7. Other good outcomes if the top goal is not reached.
8. A review date, with the question "Do you still want this, and is it still fun?"

If the user asks a direct question, such as whether to take up an offer, it answers that first.

## Child-safety rules it keeps

These are fixed and hold whatever is asked:

- A parent or guardian sends every message and holds every account. Outreach is written only for that adult to send.
- Official channels of clubs, schools and governing bodies only. No private contact between the child and an adult the family does not know.
- Nothing public about the child (profile, photo, video, school, address, schedule, location) unless the parent decides, and the most private option is always recommended. Contact details on anything public are the parent's, never the child's.
- It asks for an age band and a region, not a name, birth date, school or address, and does not repeat them if offered.
- Every opportunity is screened for five scam signs: a fee up front, a guarantee, pressure, contact with the child alone, secrecy.
- No secrecy from parents, including "surprises".
- No weight-cutting, diets, supplements or training beyond the governing body's guidance for the age. Health questions go to a doctor.
- Honest odds and no promise of making it.
- The young person's own wish to continue, change or stop comes first.
- A child asking on their own gets a warm, general answer and is asked to bring in a parent or guardian before any outreach or sign-up.

It declines requests from anyone scouting or recruiting children for a club, agency or business.

## How it differs from similar public skills

A web search on 21 November 2026 found public coaching and training skills (several named "coach" or "endurance-coach", a "practice-plan-creator", and listings titled "Youth Development" and "Sports Performance Coach"). One was read: onewave-ai/claude-skills "practice-plan-creator", which builds single practice sessions for a coach. The others were seen in search results only. None found covers the family's side of a long route, in any field, with scam screening and child-safety rules, which is this skill's ground. Nothing was copied from them.

## Example

> My daughter is 14 and wants to play basketball professionally. She plays for her school and a local club, about four hours a week. We can spend maybe $1,500 a year. Someone messaged her on Instagram offering an academy trial for a $300 registration fee.

The reply, in outline. First the offer: **do not proceed**. It came unasked, went to the child and not the parent, and asks for a fee to attend a trial; do not reply from her account, and check only through the club's official site. Then the plan: phases from local club to regional squad to a licensed academy or college route to senior basketball; the odds ("Very few players who start this route are ever paid to play, and nobody can tell at 14 who will be"); a calendar with the regional association's trials marked "to check" on its official site; a draft for the parent to email the club's published address; a routine with school, three sessions, one full day off and another sport for fun; a tracker; other good outcomes; a review in four months.

## Not for

An adult's own career change or job search; choosing a university course only; scouting or recruiting children for a club or agency; general parenting advice. It is general guidance, not legal, medical or safeguarding advice.
